Is this go for you?

You need: Go 1, or its exit check. Basic Python or R: variables, functions, a loop.

How it works: Every module starts from a connections map, explains the mechanism with a worked calculation, and ends with a small model you build and run, in Python in the browser or in R on your machine.

Quick entry check

1. In GCAM, the marketplace…

The solver moves prices until all markets clear at once, period by period.

2. A logit share rule means that…

The logit spreads the market by relative cost; the exponent sets how sharply.

3. A carbon price and an emissions cap are…

A cap is met by finding the carbon price that delivers it.

All three right? You could skip ahead to Go 3, or use this go as revision. Not sure? Revisit Go 1.

What you will produce

  • Nine small working models of GCAM's mechanisms, in Python and R
  • A note tracing one feedback between components through real GCAM output
  • Your first GCAM run read into R or Python with rgcam or gcamreader

The path

  1. 2.1Socioeconomic driversHow many people there are and how rich they become set the size of every demand in GCAM. Learn where population and GDP come from, and how the Kaya identity splits emissions into drivers.40 min
    Drivers
  2. 2.2Markets, equilibrium and solvingGCAM is a giant auction: in each 5-year period it searches for the prices that make supply equal demand in every market at once. See how one market clears, then many linked markets.45 min
    Solver
  3. 2.3Choice: the logitGCAM never gives the whole market to the cheapest option. A logit shares it out by relative cost, with share weights calibrated to history. Learn the formula, the exponent and nesting.40 min
    Choice
  4. 2.4Energy supplyWhere energy comes from in GCAM: graded fossil resources, renewable supply curves, power plants with levelised costs, and the value-factor rule that makes wind and solar dearer as they grow.50 min
    Supply
  5. 2.5Energy demand: buildings, industry, transportPeople want services, not energy: warm rooms, steel, passenger-kilometres. See how GCAM grows service demand with income and price, and how transport modes compete on money plus time.45 min
    Demand
  6. 2.6Land, food and bioenergyLand is shared among crops, pasture, forest and energy crops by a logit on profit per hectare. See why bioenergy demand raises food prices and how a carbon price can grow forests.45 min
    Land
  7. 2.7WaterGCAM balances water by river basin: cheap renewable water first, then groundwater, then desalination. See how withdrawals for farms, power and cities set the water price.35 min
    Water
  8. 2.8Emissions, climate and policy: the system togetherPut all the boxes back together. Follow emissions into Hector, see how GCAM implements carbon prices and caps, and trace five feedback loops that only an integrated model can show.60 min
    Whole system

An open course on the Global Change Analysis Model (GCAM), taught in three goes. GCAM is developed by the Joint Global Change Research Institute (PNNL); this is independent teaching material. GCAM facts follow the GCAM v8.2 documentation and the gcam-core v9.1 source; explorers and toy models are teaching versions with illustrative numbers.
