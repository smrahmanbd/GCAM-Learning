Spoken overview
Read the transcript

This module puts all the boxes back together. Emissions come from burning fuels, from land use change and from other gases such as methane and nitrous oxide. In every period GCAM passes them to Hector, a simple climate model, which turns them into concentrations, radiative forcing and global temperature. Policies close the loop. A carbon price is the simplest: you choose the price path and the model finds the emissions. An emissions cap works the other way round: you choose the emissions and the model finds the price that achieves them, and a climate target goes one step further and finds the price path that meets a temperature or forcing goal. Because everything is linked, a single policy ripples through the system. A carbon price raises bioenergy demand, which raises land prices and food prices. It also pays landowners for carbon, which grows forests, and it raises the electricity price, which shifts transport and buildings. In this module you will run a tiny integrated model with a price and a cap, and see that both doors open onto the same room.

Emissions

CO₂ = fuel use × carbon coefficients (consistent with CDIAC), plus cement process CO₂, fugitive CO₂ and land-use change CO₂. Non-CO₂ greenhouse gases (CH₄, N₂O, F-gases) use marginal abatement cost (MAC) curves that read the carbon price:

Et = At · Ft0 · (1 − MAC(pricet))

The MAC curves were updated in v8.9. Outputs: CO₂ in MtC/yr, non-CO₂ in Tg/yr; net CO₂ = CO₂ emissions by region + LUC emissions (emissions.html, outputs_emissions.html).

Climate: Hector inside GCAM

Each period GCAM passes its emissions to Hector v3.2, which computes concentrations, radiative forcing and temperature. The coupling is one-way via emissions: climate impacts on yields or runoff are not automatic in core GCAM (hector.html). The kit's toy climate model (impulse-response carbon cycle + two-layer energy balance; not Hector) gives: flat 40 Gt/yr → 637 ppm and 2.53 °C in 2100; net zero by 2050 → 443 ppm and 1.52 °C.

F = 5.35 · ln(C / C0) W/m²

In GCAM

Queries: CO2 emissions by region, global mean temperature, CO2 prices. Policy example files ship in input/policy; the course carbon price file is carbon_price_global.xml and the net-zero setup is in lab10_netzero_config_snippet.xml (policies_examples.html).

Policy: price, cap, target

A carbon price (fixedTax) is set by you; a constraint caps emissions and GCAM solves for the price; a climate target (target-finder mode, find-path=1) searches for the least-cost price path to a forcing, temperature or concentration goal and "substantially increases run time". Carbon prices are in 1990$ per tonne of carbon (policies.html, user-guide.html).

In the kit's toy system, a cap of −50% by 2050 is met by a price starting at $2.5/t in 2025 growing 5% a year: a price and a cap are two doors to the same room.

Units of the carbon price

GCAM reports carbon prices in 1990$ per tonne of carbon. Multiply by 12/44 to get $/tC from $/tCO₂, or by 44/12 the other way, and convert the dollar year before comparing with studies.

Five feedback loops

  1. Bioenergy → land → food: biomass demand raises energy-crop profit, shifting cropland and raising food prices.
  2. Carbon price → land carbon → forests: the land carbon subsidy expands forest; crops intensify.
  3. Water scarcity → cooling: rising basin prices move power from once-through to recirculating or dry cooling.
  4. Electricity price → transport and buildings: cheaper clean power pulls EVs and heat pumps in, raising electricity demand.
  5. Emissions → Hector → target: in target-finder mode the price path adjusts until the climate goal is met.

A useful mental model

Push on one box of a mobile hanging from the ceiling and every other box moves. A single carbon price moves energy, land, water and emissions together; that movement is what an integrated model is for.

Equilibrium versus optimisation

GCAM finds market equilibria period by period with no foresight. Optimisation models such as MESSAGEix choose the least-cost system over the whole century with perfect foresight; the shadow price of an emissions cap plays the role of the carbon price. In the week 11 linear program (coal, gas, solar; 100 TWh) a 40 Mt cap gives coal 22.2, gas 50, solar 27.8 TWh at a shadow price of about $22 per tonne. Both paradigms answer "what if", not "what will be".

# Module 2.8 (B) - A tiny one-region power-and-land toy IAM (illustrative only)
years <- seq(2025, 2100, 5)
cost0 <- c(coal = 60, gas = 70, solar = 80, bio_ccs = 110); ef <- c(0.9, 0.4, 0, -0.8); decline <- c(0, 0, 0.015, 0.005)
bio_land <- function(gen) gen * 3.6 / 0.35 / 180          # Mha
step <- function(t, cp, demand) {
  mix <- function(adder) { c <- cost0 * (1 - decline)^t + ef * cp; c[4] <- c[4] + adder; x <- pmax(c, 5)^-6; x / sum(x) }
  a <- uniroot(function(a) a - 0.08 * bio_land(demand * mix(a)[4]), c(0, 1e4))$root   # land market
  mix(a)
}
run <- function(prices) {
  cum <- 0; out <- data.frame()
  for (i in seq_along(years)) {
    t <- years[i] - 2025; demand <- 30000 * 1.02^t; s <- step(t, prices[i], demand); gen <- demand * s
    co2 <- sum(gen * ef) / 1000; cum <- cum + 5 * co2
    out <- rbind(out, data.frame(year = years[i], co2 = co2, solar = s[3], bio_land = bio_land(gen[4]), dT = 0.45 * cum / 1000))
  }
  out
}
ref <- run(rep(0, length(years))); tax <- run(25 * 1.05^(years - 2025))
i50 <- which(years == 2050); target <- 0.5 * ref$co2[1]
p0 <- uniroot(function(p) run(p * 1.05^(years - 2025))$co2[i50] - target, c(0.01, 5000))$root
cap <- run(p0 * 1.05^(years - 2025))
for (nm in c("Reference", "Carbon price", "Cap")) {
  r <- list(Reference = ref, "Carbon price" = tax, Cap = cap)[[nm]]
  cat(sprintf("%-13s 2050 CO2 %6.2f Gt, solar %.2f, bio land %6.1f Mha | warming added by 2100 %.2f C\n", nm, r$co2[i50], r$solar[i50], r$bio_land[i50], tail(r$dT, 1)))
}
cat(sprintf("Cap met by a price starting at %.1f $/t in 2025, growing 5%%/yr\n", p0))

Remember

  • Emissions from fuels, land and non-CO₂ gases go to Hector each period
  • A carbon price fixes the price; a cap fixes emissions and GCAM finds the price
  • Climate targets search for a price path and take much longer to run
  • One policy moves energy, land, water and emissions together through feedback loops
  • Read every result as policy minus reference, not as a forecast

Words and links

New words

Hector
A simple open-source climate model coupled to GCAM.
Radiative forcing
Extra energy trapped by greenhouse gases, in W/m².
MAC curve
Marginal abatement cost curve: how much of a gas is cut at each price.
Emissions constraint
A cap on emissions met by a solved carbon price.
Target finder
GCAM mode that searches for a price path meeting a climate target.
Shadow price
In optimisation, the cost of tightening a constraint by one unit.
