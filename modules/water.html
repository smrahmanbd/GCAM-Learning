Spoken overview
Read the transcript

Water in GCAM is tracked river basin by river basin, two hundred and thirty-five of them. Six sectors withdraw water: agriculture for irrigation, electricity for cooling power plants, manufacturing, primary energy production, livestock and municipal supply. On the supply side, each basin first uses accessible renewable water, which is cheap. When that runs short, it pumps non-renewable groundwater, which costs more, and in the last resort cities and industry can use desalination, which is expensive and uses a lot of electricity. A water price for each basin rises until demand fits supply. That price then feeds back: power plants switch to cooling systems that use less water, and farms face higher costs for irrigation, although GCAM charges irrigation only five percent of the general water price. In this module you will add up withdrawals for a basin and see how switching from dry cooling to once-through cooling can push the basin from cheap renewable water into groundwater.

يتتبع نموذج GCAM المياه حوضًا نهريًا بعد حوض، وعددها مئتان وخمسة وثلاثون حوضًا. تسحب ستة قطاعات المياه: الزراعة للري، والكهرباء لتبريد محطات التوليد، والصناعة التحويلية، وإنتاج الطاقة الأولية، والثروة الحيوانية، والإمداد البلدي. وعلى جانب العرض، يستخدم كل حوض أولًا المياه المتجددة المتاحة، وهي رخيصة. وعندما تنقص يضخ المياه الجوفية غير المتجددة، وهي أغلى، وكملاذ أخير يمكن للمدن والصناعة استخدام التحلية، وهي مكلفة وتستهلك كثيرًا من الكهرباء. ويرتفع سعر المياه في كل حوض حتى يتسع العرض للطلب. ثم يعود هذا السعر ليؤثر: فتنتقل محطات الكهرباء إلى أنظمة تبريد أقل استهلاكًا للمياه، وتواجه المزارع تكاليف أعلى للري، مع أن النموذج يحمّل الري خمسة بالمئة فقط من السعر العام للمياه. في هذه الوحدة ستجمع سحوبات المياه لحوض ما، وترى كيف يمكن أن يدفع التحول من التبريد الجاف إلى التبريد بالتدفق المفتوح الحوضَ من المياه المتجددة الرخيصة إلى المياه الجوفية.

What this component represents

GCAM tracks water by 235 basins (common_assumptions.html). Agricultural demand is modelled directly per basin; other sectors are modelled by region and mapped to basins. Demand is reported as withdrawals, consumption, biophysical consumption and seawater (demand_water.html).

Six demand sectors

Agriculture (irrigation), electricity generation, manufacturing, primary energy, livestock and municipal. Irrigation = coefficient (km³ per Mt) × crop production. Municipal per-capita demand follows

pcWt = pcWt−1 · (pcGDP ratio)0.37 · (price ratio)−0.33 · (1 − Techt)

Power plants choose among five cooling systems by a cost logit; water coefficients come from Macknick et al. (2011) (demand_water.html).

Three supply tiers and the water price

Renewable water comes from Xanthos runoff. Accessible water is

QA = max(0, min(QT − EFR, QB − EFR + RS))

with streamflow QT, baseflow QB, environmental flow requirement EFR and reservoir storage RS. Beyond it, non-renewable groundwater on graded curves, then desalination. A basin price in 1975$/m³ adjusts until demand equals supply; irrigation pays 5% of it (water.IRR_PRICE_SUBSIDY_MULT) (supply_water.html).

In GCAM

Query water withdrawals by region and water withdrawals by basin (course query file). Map basins with rmap::mapGCAMBasins. The irrigation price multiplier is water.IRR_PRICE_SUBSIDY_MULT in gcamdata's constants.

Worked example: cooling decides the tier

Kit toy basin: 50 km³ of renewable water at $0.01/m³, 30 km³ of groundwater at $0.05/m³, then desalination at $0.80/m³. Irrigation 40 km³ and municipal 8 km³. Power at 300 TWh with dry-ish cooling (2 L/kWh) adds 0.6 km³ → total 48.6 km³, price $0.01/m³. With once-through cooling (60 L/kWh) power adds 18 km³ → 66.0 km³, which exceeds renewable supply: the price jumps to $0.05/m³.

Withdrawal is not consumption

A once-through cooled plant withdraws a lot but returns most of it warmer; a recirculating plant withdraws less but evaporates more of what it takes. Always say which measure you report.

Water–energy–land links

Energy for water: desalination by reverse osmosis uses 2.75 kWh/m³, thermal desalination 58.3, groundwater pumping 0.185 (demand_energy.html). Water for energy: primary energy production withdraws water (withdrawal/consumption ratio 3.3; seawater share 95% in the Middle East). Water for land: water costs enter crop profit, shifting irrigated versus rainfed land.

Gulf angle

In the Middle East region GCAM assumes 95% seawater for primary energy production, and desalination ties city water to electricity demand: a strong water–energy coupling for Saudi Arabia studies.

# Module 2.7 - Water: basin supply tiers and water for power cooling (toy numbers)
water_price <- function(w, renewable = 50, ground = 30)
  if (w <= renewable) 0.01 else if (w <= renewable + ground) 0.05 else 0.8      # $ per m3
withdrawals <- function(irrigation, municipal, power_twh, l_per_kwh = 2) irrigation + municipal + power_twh * 1e9 * l_per_kwh / 1e12
for (x in list(c(100, 2), c(300, 2), c(300, 60))) {
  w <- withdrawals(40, 8, x[1], x[2])
  cat(sprintf("%d TWh at %g L/kWh: %.1f km3 -> $%g per m3\n", x[1], x[2], w, water_price(w)))
}

"""Go 2 - pull the handbook's GCAM results into pandas with gcamreader.

NOT RUN AGAINST A LIVE GCAM DATABASE. Written to the gcamreader API as documented
(gcamreader 1.5.0, Python >= 3.11, needs Java): LocalDBConn, parse_batch_query, runQuery.
Install:  pip install gcamreader
Usage:    python read_gcam.py /path/to/gcam/output database_basexdb
"""
import sys
import gcamreader

db_dir = sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else "../gcam/output"     # folder that holds the database
db_name = sys.argv[2] if len(sys.argv) > 2 else "database_basexdb"

conn = gcamreader.LocalDBConn(db_dir, db_name)
queries = gcamreader.parse_batch_query("go2_queries.xml")             # the 8 course queries
print("queries in file:", [q.title for q in queries])

wanted = {  # handbook module -> query title
    "2.1 / 2.8": "CO2 emissions by region",
    "2.4": "elec gen by subsector",
    "2.4 / 2.5": "primary energy consumption by region (direct equivalent)",
    "2.8": "global mean temperature",
    "2.8 ": "CO2 prices",
    "2.6": "aggregated land allocation",
    "2.7": "water withdrawals by region",
    "2.7 ": "water withdrawals by basin",
}
for module, title in wanted.items():
    q = next((q for q in queries if q.title == title), None)
    if q is None:
        print("missing query:", title); continue
    df = conn.runQuery(q)                     # optional: scenarios=["Reference", "CarbonPrice"]
    out = title.replace(" ", "_").replace("(", "").replace(")", "") + ".csv"
    df.to_csv(out, index=False)
    print(f"module {module.strip():<9} {title:<58} -> {out} ({len(df)} rows)")

Words and links

New words

Withdrawal
Water taken from a river, lake, aquifer or the sea.
Consumption
The part of a withdrawal that is not returned to the source.
Environmental flow requirement
Water left in rivers to protect ecosystems.
Xanthos
The global hydrology model that supplies GCAM's runoff.
Superwell
The groundwater extraction cost model behind GCAM's groundwater grades.
