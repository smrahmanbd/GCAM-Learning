Research brief

In this module you will

  • Extract transport results with saved queries into tidy tables
  • Report every policy result as a difference from Reference
  • Decompose a change in transport CO2 into activity, structure, intensity and carbon effects
  • Stress-test findings with a tornado chart and scenario discovery, and benchmark against AR6
Spoken overview
Read the transcript

A result is only as good as the way you get it out and test it. Step one: use the same saved queries every time, and save tidy tables, so that every figure can be rebuilt. Step two: never report a policy run alone. Report the difference from the Reference run, and explain it. Step three: decompose the change. The logarithmic mean Divisia index splits a change in transport carbon dioxide into four parts that add up exactly: more travel, a shift between modes, less energy per passenger-kilometre, and less carbon per unit of energy. Step four: stress-test. A tornado chart shows which single assumption moves the result most. An ensemble with scenario discovery finds the combinations of inputs that lead to high emissions. In the course kit's toy ensemble, the battery electric vehicle cost explained most high-emission runs, followed by the carbon price. Step five: benchmark against other models, for example the IPCC's AR6 scenario database, and explain any differences.

Step 1: get the numbers out, the same way every time

Why this matters

In the kit's toy transport model, filling cars with more people (the LF-up scenario) should cut emissions. By 2050 it cuts them by only 3 Mt out of 84 — because cheaper car travel makes people travel 15 % more and pulls them out of buses. The first number you look at is rarely the answer; the analysis is in explaining it.

Research note

Two toy artefacts worth noticing. (1) In the toy Reference, the BEV share jumps to about half of car pkm in 2030, because the share-weight switches from 0 to 1 between 2025 and 2030 and the toy has no vintages. GCAM smooths this with an s-curve and capital stock. (2) Sales-like shares ("tech (new)") move much faster than the stock of vehicles on the road (Figure 3.15 in module 3.7). Always say which one you report.

Step 3: decompose the change

To explain why transport CO2 differs between two years or two scenarios, split it into factors, exactly like the Kaya identity in Go 1 but for transport. For each mode m:

CO2 = Σm A · Sm · Im · Fm(3.8)

with A total activity (pkm), Sm the mode share, Im energy per pkm and Fm CO2 per unit of energy. The logarithmic mean Divisia index (LMDI) attributes the change to each factor with no leftover residual:

ΔCO2(X) = Σm L(Cm1, Cm0) · ln(Xm1 ⁄ Xm0),    L(a, b) = (a − b) ⁄ (ln a − ln b)(3.9)

Worked calculation

LMDI for an illustrative country (three modes, two years)

  1. CO2 falls from 19.36 to 13.13 Mt: a change of -6.24 Mt.
  2. Activity (pkm 200 → 300 bn): +6.49 Mt.
  3. Mode structure (cars 80 % → 70 %, rail 5 % → 15 %): -1.72 Mt.
  4. Energy intensity (cars 1.6 → 1.1 MJ/pkm, etc.): -5.71 Mt.
  5. Carbon intensity (fuel switching, cleaner electricity): -5.30 Mt.
  6. Sum of effects: -6.24 Mt — equal to the total change, with no residual.

Answer: intensity and carbon each cut more than mode shift; activity growth eats most of the gains

Step 4: stress-test

One at a time: the tornado

Move each uncertain input from its low to its high value with everything else central, and plot the range of the output. The longest bar is the input your conclusion depends on most. It is cheap (two runs per input) but misses interactions.

Many at once: ensembles and scenario discovery

Run a full factorial or Latin-hypercube ensemble in batch mode. Label each run "high" or "not high" for the outcome you care about (for example, 2050 transport CO2 above a target). Then search for a simple box in input space that captures the high runs — with the PRIM algorithm or a classification tree (lab 13 uses rpart). Judge the box with two numbers:

coverage = (high runs inside the box) ⁄ (all high runs),    density = (high runs inside the box) ⁄ (all runs inside the box)(3.10)

Read the output like this: the tree splits first on BEV cost, and the PRIM-style box keeps 80 % of the high runs with 92 % of runs inside it high (against a base rate of 25 %). The car load factor and GDP growth were never used — in this synthetic ensemble, they do not explain failure. With real GCAM runs, check that the box is stable when you re-sample.

Worked calculation

Scenario discovery on 36 runs

  1. 9 of the 36 runs miss the 2050 target ("high").
  2. Candidate box: "BEV cost high AND share-weight path slow" contains 10 runs, of which 7 are high.
  3. Coverage = 7 ÷ 9 = 0.78: the box explains 78 % of the failures.
  4. Density = 7 ÷ 10 = 0.70: inside the box, 70 % of runs fail.
  5. A good box has both high; narrowing the box raises density but lowers coverage — report the trade-off.

Answer: coverage 0.78, density 0.70

Step 5: benchmark against other models

Put your Reference and policy runs next to the AR6 scenario database (or the newer CMIP7 ScenarioMIP set) for the same region and variables: Final Energy|Transportation, Emissions|CO2|Energy|Demand|Transportation, Energy Service|Transportation|Passenger. Lab 12 shows how to filter AR6 runs to a similar carbon price. Being outside the range is not wrong, but you must be able to say why (module 3.3).

Pitfall

Reporting a percentage of the wrong base. "Transport CO2 falls 60 %" — below what? The 2020 level, or the 2050 Reference? The two can differ by a factor of two when Reference emissions grow. Always say "relative to Reference in the same year" or "relative to 2020".

"""decompose_lmdi.py - LMDI-I additive decomposition of passenger-transport CO2.

  CO2 = sum over modes m of  A x S_m x I_m x C_m
    A   activity          total passenger-km (all modes, incl. walk and cycle)
    S_m structure         mode share of passenger-km
    I_m energy intensity  EJ per passenger-km of mode m
    C_m carbon intensity  tCO2 per EJ of mode m (direct, from the fuel mix)
  Effect of factor X = sum_m L(CO2_m^1, CO2_m^0) x ln(X_m^1 / X_m^0),
  L(a, b) = (a - b) / (ln a - ln b) (logarithmic mean; L(a, a) = a). The four effects add up
  EXACTLY to the change in CO2 (Ang 2005, Energy Policy 33:867-871; Ang 2015 Energy Policy 86:233-238).
  Zero values are replaced by 1e-12 (Ang & Liu 2007 recommend a small number), so modes with no
  direct CO2 (rail on electricity) contribute nothing.

Two uses: over time (2020 -> 2050 within a scenario) and across scenarios (Reference -> policy in 2050).
Direct emission factors (tCO2/GJ): refined liquids 19.6 kgC/GJ, gas 14.2 kgC/GJ (gcam-core
input/gcamdata/inst/extdata/energy/A_PrimaryFuelCCoef.csv), electricity and hydrogen 0 at the tailpipe.
Input: analysis/out/{service_by_mode,energy_by_mode_fuel}.csv from read_transport.py.
Run: python decompose_lmdi.py   (writes out/lmdi_results.csv)
"""
import math, os
import pandas as pd

HERE = os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__))
EF = {"refined liquids enduse": 19.6 * 44 / 12 / 1000, "delivered gas": 14.2 * 44 / 12 / 1000}  # tCO2/GJ
EPS = 1e-12


def L(a, b):
    a, b = max(a, EPS), max(b, EPS)
    return a if abs(a - b) < 1e-15 else (a - b) / (math.log(a) - math.log(b))


def factors(svc, en, scen, year):
    s = svc[(svc.scenario == scen) & (svc.year == year)].groupby("mode").value.sum()
    e = en[(en.scenario == scen) & (en.year == year)].copy()
    e["co2"] = e.apply(lambda r: r.value * 1e3 * EF.get(r.fuel, 0.0), axis=1)     # EJ -> Mt CO2
    ej = e.groupby("mode").value.sum(); co2 = e.groupby("mode").co2.sum()
    A = s.sum(); out = {}
    for m in ej.index:
        out[m] = dict(A=A, S=s[m] / A, I=ej[m] / s[m], C=(co2[m] / ej[m]) if ej[m] > 0 else 0.0, CO2=co2[m])
    return out


def lmdi(f0, f1):
    eff = dict(activity=0.0, structure=0.0, intensity=0.0, carbon=0.0)
    for m in set(f0) | set(f1):
        a = f0.get(m, dict(A=1, S=EPS, I=EPS, C=EPS, CO2=0.0)); b = f1.get(m, dict(A=1, S=EPS, I=EPS, C=EPS, CO2=0.0))
        if a["CO2"] <= 0 and b["CO2"] <= 0:
            continue
        w = L(b["CO2"], a["CO2"])
        for k, x in (("activity", "A"), ("structure", "S"), ("intensity", "I"), ("carbon", "C")):
            eff[k] += w * math.log(max(b[x], EPS) / max(a[x], EPS))
    tot0 = sum(v["CO2"] for v in f0.values()); tot1 = sum(v["CO2"] for v in f1.values())
    eff.update(co2_start=tot0, co2_end=tot1, change=tot1 - tot0, residual=tot1 - tot0 - sum(eff[k] for k in ("activity", "structure", "intensity", "carbon")))
    return eff


def main():
    svc = pd.read_csv(os.path.join(HERE, "out", "service_by_mode.csv"))
    en = pd.read_csv(os.path.join(HERE, "out", "energy_by_mode_fuel.csv"))
    rows = []
    for sc in sorted(svc.scenario.unique()):
        rows.append(dict(comparison=f"{sc}: 2020 -> 2050", **lmdi(factors(svc, en, sc, 2020), factors(svc, en, sc, 2050))))
    for sc in sorted(svc.scenario.unique()):
        if sc != "Reference":
            rows.append(dict(comparison=f"2050: Reference -> {sc}", **lmdi(factors(svc, en, "Reference", 2050), factors(svc, en, sc, 2050))))
    df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    pd.set_option("display.width", 160)
    print(df.round(2).to_string(index=False))
    print(f"\nlargest |residual| = {df.residual.abs().max():.2e} Mt (LMDI is exact up to rounding)")
    df.to_csv(os.path.join(HERE, "out", "lmdi_results.csv"), index=False)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
Applies the decomposition to query-shaped tables (sample data in the kit). Kit result for Reference 2020→2050: activity +41.2, structure +7.7, intensity −50.1, carbon −47.9, total −49.0 Mt.

Reading list

New words

LMDI
Logarithmic mean Divisia index: an exact decomposition of a change into contributing factors.
Tornado chart
Bars showing how much the result moves when each input goes from low to high, one at a time.
Coverage
Share of all outcomes of interest that a scenario-discovery box captures.
Density
Share of runs inside a scenario-discovery box that are outcomes of interest.
Benchmarking
Comparing results with other models or databases to check plausibility.
