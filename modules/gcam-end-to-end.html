Read the transcript

Now meet one model from end to end. GCAM, the Global Change Analysis Model, is built at the Pacific Northwest National Laboratory and is free and open source. It splits the world into thirty two energy and economy regions, more than three hundred land units and two hundred and thirty five river basins. It starts from a calibrated base year, twenty twenty one, and then steps forward five years at a time to twenty one hundred. In each step it acts like an auctioneer: it adjusts the prices of fuels, crops, land and water until supply equals demand in every market at once. It does not look ahead; each period is solved in turn. Energy, land and water are linked. Bioenergy needs land, power plants need cooling water, and a carbon price reaches forests too. Emissions go to Hector, a simple climate model, which returns the temperature. How do you read a GCAM result? Never alone. Every study runs a reference scenario, with no new climate policy, and one or more policy scenarios with exactly the same inputs plus one change. The effect of the policy is the difference. A policy can enter as a carbon price, a toll, or as an emissions limit, a gate. The model finds the cut for the toll, or the price for the gate. Finally, remember what models cannot tell you: who pays, how people really behave, and whether the assumptions will come true.

GCAM in one paragraph

GCAM, the Global Change Analysis Model, comes from the Pacific Northwest National Laboratory's Joint Global Change Research Institute (PNNL/JGCRI) and is free and open source. It splits the world into 32 energy-economy regions, more than 300 land units and 235 river basins. It starts from a calibrated 2021 base year and steps forward five years at a time to 2100. Each step is solved on its own: the model has no foresight (it is recursive-dynamic).

Facts here follow the GCAM v8.2 documentation; the latest release is GCAM 9.1 (2 June 2026). The overview page still says 2015 for the base year, which is out of date.

In GCAM

Regions, basins and time steps are set in the input files built by GCAM's R data system (gcamdata). In Go 2 you will read results with the R package rgcam or the Python package gcamreader.

An auction in every period

In each period GCAM behaves like an auctioneer: it adjusts the prices of fuels, crops, land and water until supply equals demand in every market at once. Technologies and land uses compete by cost and share each market. Emissions are counted from the fuels burned and land changed, and handed to Hector, a simple climate model, which returns temperature.

Read results as Policy minus Reference

A GCAM result is never read alone. A study runs a Reference scenario (no new climate policy) and one or more policy scenarios with exactly the same inputs plus one change. The effect of the policy is the difference between the two runs. A reference is a yardstick, not a forecast.

Common trap

"GCAM has a button for each country's climate pledge." It does not: turning a pledge into a price or a limit is the modeller's choice, and a good report says how it was done. And "the models disagree, so they are useless" is wrong: the reasons they disagree are findings in themselves.

A toll or a gate: price and cap are mirrors

A policy can enter the model in two ways. A carbon price is a toll: you fix the cost per tonne and the model finds how much emissions fall. An emissions limit is a gate: you fix the emissions and the model finds the price that achieves it. On the same cost curve they are mirror images.

Illustrative straight-line cost curve (price = 2 × cut%): a $50 toll gives a 25% cut; a gate requiring a 60% cut needs a $120 price.

Try it now

Your friend says "GCAM showed that a $50 carbon price cuts emissions by 25%." Write the two questions you would ask before believing it. (Compared with which Reference, and what did it assume? Which assumptions, such as costs and demand, matter most, and was there a sensitivity test?)

What IAMs cannot tell you

  • Who pays and who gains: regions are large and households are averaged.
  • How people really behave: choices follow costs and calibrated preferences, not politics or habits.
  • Whether the assumptions will come true: results lean on assumptions such as future costs and the availability of carbon removal.

Reviews of IAM criticisms list heavy reliance on carbon removal later in the century, limited transparency and limited behavioural realism. The fair conclusion is not that models are useless, but that their findings are conditional, so keep the model, question the inputs.

Remember

  • GCAM: 32 regions, 300+ land units, 235 basins; 2021 base year, then 5-year steps to 2100.
  • Each period, prices adjust until every market clears; there is no foresight.
  • Energy, land and water are linked; emissions go to Hector, which returns temperature.
  • Read results as Policy minus Reference; a reference is a yardstick, not a forecast.
  • A carbon price (toll) and an emissions limit (gate) are mirrors on the same cost curve.
  • IAM findings are conditional: question the assumptions, not just the results.

Check yourself

1. How many energy-economy regions does GCAM use?

32 regions. 235 is the number of river basins and 'more than 300' is the number of land units.

2. 'Recursive-dynamic' means GCAM:

GCAM clears markets one period at a time and does not anticipate the future.

3. The effect of a policy in GCAM should be read as:

Everything else is identical, so the difference isolates the policy's effect.

4. In GCAM, a $50 carbon price is an example of:

With a price policy you set the price (a toll) and GCAM works out how far emissions fall.

5. Which is NOT something an IAM can reliably tell you?

IAMs average households over large regions and model behaviour through costs; detailed behaviour is outside their reach.

Words and links

New words

GCAM
Global Change Analysis Model: an open-source, process-based IAM from PNNL/JGCRI linking economy, energy, land, water and climate.
Recursive-dynamic
Solving one period at a time without foresight, as GCAM does.
Base year
The calibrated starting year of the model; 2021 in recent GCAM versions.
Reference scenario
A run with no new climate policy, used as the yardstick for policy runs.
Hector
The simple climate model inside GCAM that turns emissions into concentrations, forcing and temperature.
Emissions constraint
A policy that fixes emissions; the model finds the carbon price that meets it.
