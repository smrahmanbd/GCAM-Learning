Uncertainty: reading a range, not a line

Every model result rests on uncertain inputs. Running many versions, or many models, turns one line into a range. Learn to report and read that range honestly.

Spoken overview
Read the transcript

No model can tell us exactly what will happen. Its results depend on inputs we are unsure about: how fast technology costs fall, how the economy grows, how people behave, and how strongly the climate responds to carbon dioxide. There are three main kinds of uncertainty. Parameter uncertainty is about the numbers inside a model. Structural uncertainty is about how the model is built, which is why different models give different answers to the same question. Scenario uncertainty is about choices that society has not made yet. To handle the first two, modellers run ensembles: the same model many times with different inputs, or many models with the same inputs. Instead of one line you get a band. Report the middle, called the median, and a range, such as the likely range covering two thirds of runs. You can also report the chance of crossing a threshold, for example a sixty percent chance of exceeding two degrees. Scenario uncertainty is handled differently: we do not attach probabilities to choices, we compare them. A good reader of model results always asks: what was varied, what was held fixed, and how wide is the band?

Three kinds of uncertainty

Parameter: the numbers inside a model, such as how much warming per tonne of CO2 (about 0.27–0.63 °C per 1,000 Gt, likely range). Structural: how the model is built; GCAM, MESSAGEix and REMIND can give different answers to the same question. Scenario: what society chooses, such as policy and lifestyles.

Ensembles turn a line into a band

A perturbed-parameter ensemble runs one model many times with inputs drawn from their ranges. A multi-model ensemble runs several models on the same scenario, as in the IPCC AR6 scenario database. Either way you get a distribution of results.

Reading the range

The median is the middle run. The likely range covers the middle two thirds (17th to 83rd percentile). A probability such as "a 60% chance of exceeding 2 °C" is the share of runs above 2 °C, and only holds under the ensemble's assumptions.

Common trap

"The models agree, so it is certain." Models can share assumptions and data. Agreement narrows parameter and structural doubt; it does not remove scenario uncertainty.

Scenarios are not forecasts

We do not say "SSP3 has a 20% chance". Scenarios describe choices; we compare them, each with its own uncertainty range. When you read a figure, ask three things: what was varied, what was held fixed, and how wide is the band?

Remember

  • Results rest on uncertain parameters, model structures and choices.
  • Ensembles turn one line into a band.
  • Report a median, a likely range and, if useful, a probability.
  • Compare scenarios side by side; do not give them probabilities.

Words and links

New words

Ensemble
A set of model runs that differ in inputs or in the model used.
Median
The middle value: half the runs are above it, half below.
Likely range
In IPCC usage, a range with about a two-in-three chance of containing the outcome.
Structural uncertainty
Uncertainty from how a model is built, revealed by comparing different models.
