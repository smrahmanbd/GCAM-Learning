Spoken overview
Read the transcript

GCAM is not the only integrated assessment model, and a good researcher knows its relatives. The models differ in three big choices. The first is foresight. GCAM, IMAGE and AIM-Hub are recursive: they solve one period at a time without knowing the future. MESSAGEix, REMIND and WITCH have perfect foresight: they choose every period at once. The second is how much of the economy they cover. GCAM and IMAGE are partial equilibrium models of energy and land markets, while AIM-Hub, REMIND and WITCH represent the whole economy. The third is how they solve. MESSAGEix minimises total system cost with a large linear programme. REMIND maximises welfare over the century. WITCH lets regions play a game against each other. IMAGE simulates land and the environment on a fine grid. DICE, the simplest, weighs the cost of cutting emissions against the damage avoided. These choices shape results. Foresight models tend to act early and smoothly, with carbon prices rising at the discount rate. Recursive models react period by period. In transport, REMIND's EDGE-T module is a fork of GCAM's transport model. Open the model atlas to explore each one.

Three choices that define a model

Foresight: recursive (myopic) models solve each period with today's information; intertemporal models optimise all periods together. Economic scope: partial equilibrium (energy, land, water markets, with GDP given) or general equilibrium (all markets, GDP responds). Solution method: market clearing, cost minimisation, welfare maximisation, simulation or a game.

How each one solves

  • GCAM: in each 5-year period, a solver adjusts prices until every market clears; technologies share markets by logit.
  • MESSAGEix: one linear programme picks all technologies in all periods to meet demand at least discounted cost; a constraint's shadow price is the carbon price.
  • REMIND: a social planner maximises discounted welfare; investment in energy competes with consumption.
  • IMAGE: simulates year by year; land demand is allocated to grid cells by suitability.
  • AIM-Hub: all goods and factor markets clear each year; policy cost shows up as GDP or consumption loss.
  • WITCH: each region optimises given the others' choices until no one wants to change (Nash equilibrium), or a coalition optimises jointly.
  • DICE: cuts until the marginal cost of abatement equals the social cost of carbon.

Structure shapes results

Foresight models under a carbon budget tend to produce carbon prices that rise at about the discount rate (the Hotelling rule) and act early where it is cheap. Recursive models respond to the price path you give them. General-equilibrium models report GDP losses; partial-equilibrium models report the area under a cost curve. Logit models spread markets across technologies; pure LP models can switch abruptly unless constrained. When two models disagree, check these differences before blaming the data.

Common trap

"Model A says $200 per tonne, model B $80, so one is wrong." A GDP-loss cost and an area-under-curve cost are different measures, and foresight changes the timing. Compare like with like.

Transport across the family

GCAM represents passenger and freight transport by mode, size class and technology with a value-of-time cost and logit choice (module 3.2). REMIND couples EDGE-T, which its developers describe as a fork of the GCAM transport module, run between REMIND iterations. MESSAGEix has a detailed MESSAGEix-Transport variant. IMAGE's TIMER model includes a passenger transport module. This shared ancestry is one reason transport results from GCAM and REMIND can be compared closely.

Research idea

Run the same transport policy (say, a ZEV standard) in GCAM and compare with published REMIND-EDGE-T results: where do the logit settings and value of time explain the gap?

Choosing a model, or several

Ask what your question needs. Policy what-ifs with rich energy-land-water detail and open code: GCAM. Least-cost pathways and investment timing: MESSAGEix or REMIND. Economy-wide costs and trade: AIM-Hub or another CGE. Strategic behaviour between regions: WITCH. The optimal carbon price: DICE. For a robust finding, use a model comparison with harmonised inputs, as in the EMF and IPCC exercises.

Reading list

New words

Recursive dynamic
Solving one period at a time using only current information (myopic).
Perfect foresight
Choosing all periods together, knowing future prices and policies.
Computable general equilibrium (CGE)
A model in which all markets in the economy, including labour and capital, clear together.
Hotelling rule
On a least-cost path under a fixed budget, the carbon price rises at the discount rate.
Nash equilibrium
A set of choices where no player gains by changing its own choice alone.
Social cost of carbon
The present value of damages from emitting one more tonne of CO2.
