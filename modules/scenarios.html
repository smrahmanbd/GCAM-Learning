Since about twenty sixteen the research community has used two labels together. The first is the shared socioeconomic pathway, or SSP. There are five stories about how society develops: sustainability, middle of the road, regional rivalry, inequality, and fossil-fuelled development. They differ in how hard they make it to cut emissions and to adapt. The second label is a number: how much extra heat the atmosphere traps in twenty one hundred, in watts per square metre. So SSP two four point five means the middle-of-the-road story pushed to four point five watts per square metre, which leads to roughly two point seven degrees of warming. Not every combination works. A fossil-fuelled world can reach low forcing only with huge effort, and in the regional rivalry story most models could not reach the lowest levels at all. For its twenty twenty two report the IPCC collected about three thousand pathways and sorted those it could assess into eight categories, from C1, one point five degrees with little or no overshoot, to C8, above four degrees. What a scenario is

A scenario is a consistent what-if story with numbers stapled to it, like a choose-your-own-adventure book with a spreadsheet on every page. It is not a forecast, and a set of scenarios is not a list of odds.

Two labels: a story and a number

Since about 2016 the community has used two labels together. The Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs) are five stories about how society develops. They are placed by how hard they make it to cut emissions (mitigation) and to adapt.

The second label is the radiative forcing in 2100, in W/m², inherited from the older Representative Concentration Pathways (RCPs). Higher number, more warming.

Decode SSP2-4.5: SSP2 is the middle-of-the-road story; 4.5 is forcing in 2100 of 4.5 W/m², not 4.5 °C. It leads to roughly 2.7 °C of warming (check against IPCC AR6 WGI before teaching).

Try it now

Decode SSP5-8.5 and SSP1-2.6 in one sentence each. Which would you call a reference, and why is that word risky? (SSP5-8.5: fossil-fuelled growth reaching 8.5 W/m², a very high-emission world. SSP1-2.6: a sustainable world reaching 2.6 W/m². 'Reference' means 'no new policy', which is a modelling choice, not a prediction.)

The scenario matrix

Put the stories in rows and the forcing levels in columns and you get a matrix. The IPCC's 2021 climate report used five headline cells: SSP1-1.9, SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, SSP3-7.0 and SSP5-8.5.

Not every cell can be reached. In SSP3's world of regional rivalry, models could not reach the lowest forcing levels. And forcing above a story's no-policy level is not a policy case at all.

Common trap

"30 of 40 models show it, so it is 75% likely." No. Models are not a random sample of futures, and they share data and code. A spread across models describes the tools, not the odds. And the 4.5 in SSP2-4.5 is not degrees.

How the IPCC sorts pathways

For its 2022 mitigation report the IPCC collected about 3,000 pathways and sorted 1,202 into eight categories by the warming they lead to: C1 limits warming to 1.5 °C (over 50% chance) with little or no overshoot; C3 keeps it below 2 °C (over 67% chance); C8 exceeds 4 °C. Check category wording against AR6 WGIII Box SPM.1 before teaching.

Scenarios get updated. In 2026 a new set of seven scenarios for the next generation of climate models (CMIP7) was published, each produced by a different IAM team; the high one, H, came from GCAM.

Remember

  • A scenario is a consistent what-if story with numbers, not a forecast or a probability.
  • SSP = story about society; the number = forcing in 2100 in W/m², not °C.
  • Some SSP × forcing cells cannot be reached, for example SSP3 at the lowest levels.
  • The IPCC's 2022 report sorted pathways into categories C1 (1.5 °C) to C8 (above 4 °C).
  • CMIP7 (2026) brings seven new scenarios, each from one IAM; GCAM made the high one.

Words and links

New words

Scenario
A consistent what-if story about the future with numbers attached; not a forecast.
SSP
Shared Socioeconomic Pathway: one of five stories about how society develops, used as IAM inputs.
RCP
Representative Concentration Pathway: an earlier scenario set named by its forcing in 2100; the forcing numbers live on in SSP labels.
Scenario matrix
A grid with SSP stories in rows and forcing levels in columns.
AR6 category
One of eight IPCC classes (C1 to C8) that sort pathways by the warming they lead to.
CMIP7
The next round of the climate-model intercomparison; its 2026 scenarios each come from a different IAM.
