Spoken overview
Read the transcript

Every number that comes out of GCAM starts with two quiet assumptions: how many people there will be, and how rich they will become. Population comes from the Shared Socioeconomic Pathways, a set of five stories about the future. Income comes from labour productivity growth, and together they give gross domestic product for each of GCAM's thirty-two regions. These drivers then push on everything else. Richer people want more floor space, more travel, more meat and more water, so demand in every sector grows with income per person. In this module you will grow population and income for two invented storylines, a green road and a rocky road, and see how far apart their emissions end up. You will use the Kaya identity, which writes emissions as people times income per person times energy per dollar times carbon per unit of energy. The four factors multiply, they do not add, so small changes compound over eighty years. Finally, you will meet GCAM's macro module, which since version seven can let energy prices feed back into GDP itself.

What this component represents

The socioeconomic drivers are the number of people and their income, region by region and period by period. They are not results of the energy or land model in the standard setup: they are assumptions you choose, usually from one of the five Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs).

GCAM uses 32 geopolitical regions and steps forward in 5-year periods from 2025 to 2100, after a base year of 2021. Population and GDP set the overall scale of activity that every other module must serve (overview.html, common_assumptions.html).

How GCAM builds population and GDP

The economy module reads a small set of exogenous inputs: population (thousands), labour productivity growth, labour force participation, base-year GDP and national accounts (economy.html). Population before 2015 comes from Maddison and the UN; from 2015 the SSP database supplies it. Future GDP comes from the SSP database (OECD model).

GDPr,t = populationr,t × labour force share × output per workerr,t
output per workert = output per workert−1 × (1 + g)5

Here g is the yearly labour productivity growth rate. Five years of growth compound in each model step.

In GCAM

Population and GDP inputs are built by gcamdata from SSP_database_2024.csv.gz, USDA_GDP_MER.csv and pwt91.csv (inputs_economy.html). In a run, query GDP by region and Population by region in ModelInterface, or with rgcam/gcamreader. All five SSPs are implemented; GCAM was the marker model for SSP4 (ssp.html).

Income drives every demand

GDP per person is the bridge to the rest of the model. Buildings floorspace follows a Gompertz curve in income, so it rises fast and then saturates. Industry and transport service output follow

Dt = Dt−1 · (Yt/Yt−1)α · (Pt/Pt−1)β · (Nt/Nt−1)

where Y is GDP per person, P the service price, N population, α the income elasticity and β the price elasticity (demand_energy.html). Food demand, municipal water (income elasticity 0.37) and air-pollution controls also depend on income (demand_land.html, demand_water.html).

The Kaya identity: splitting emissions into drivers

The Kaya identity writes CO2 as a product of four factors. It is always true, because the units cancel:

CO2 = population × (GDP / person) × (energy / GDP) × (CO2 / energy)

Because the factors multiply, their ratios multiply too. In the kit's invented Green road, population grows 1.10×, income per person 5.70×, energy per dollar falls to 0.132× and carbon per unit of energy to 0.087×. The product is 0.072: emissions in 2100 are 7% of 2020. On the Rocky road the ratios are 1.52, 2.22, 0.53 and 0.79, giving 1.393, a 39% rise.

Adding instead of multiplying

Avalon's factors change by +20%, +50%, −30% and −20%. Adding gives +20%, which is wrong. Multiply: 1.2 × 1.5 × 0.7 × 0.8 = 1.008, so CO2 rises by only 0.8%.

When GDP stops being fixed: the macro module

Since GCAM v7 a macroeconomic module (KLEM: capital, labour, energy, materials) can make GDP respond to the energy system. A nested CES production function uses an energy-service efficiency index from the energy module, and capital accumulates with depreciation (economy.html). In open-GDP mode a carbon policy changes GDP and consumption directly. GCAM v9.0 extended it to KLEAM, adding agriculture with its own labour and capital. The CES parameter values are not given on the documentation pages.

Version note

The online documentation describes GCAM v8.2; the latest release is GCAM 9.1 (2 June 2026), which includes KLEAM (v9.0). The overview page still calls 2015 the final calibration year; other pages and the v8.0 notes say 2021, which is current.

# Module 2.1 - Socioeconomic drivers: population, GDP and the Kaya identity (toy numbers)
years <- seq(2020, 2100, by = 5); t <- years - 2020
population <- function(p0, pmax, rate) pmax / (1 + (pmax / p0 - 1) * exp(-rate * t))   # billion
income <- function(y0, growth) y0 * (1 + growth)^t                                     # thousand $ per person

stories <- list(
  "Green road" = list(pop = population(7.8, 8.6, 0.05), inc = income(11, 0.022), ei = -0.025, ci = -0.030),
  "Rocky road" = list(pop = population(7.8, 12.5, 0.03), inc = income(11, 0.010), ei = -0.008, ci = -0.003))

par(mfrow = c(1, 3))
res <- list()
for (nm in names(stories)) {
  s <- stories[[nm]]
  gdp <- s$pop * s$inc                       # trillion $
  e_gdp <- 6.0 * (1 + s$ei)^t                # MJ per $
  c_e <- 0.065 * (1 + s$ci)^t                # kg CO2 per MJ
  co2 <- gdp * e_gdp * c_e                   # Gt CO2
  r <- c(pop = s$pop[17] / s$pop[1], income = s$inc[17] / s$inc[1], e_gdp = e_gdp[17] / e_gdp[1], c_e = c_e[17] / c_e[1])
  cat(nm, ": ratios 2100/2020 =", round(r, 3), " product =", round(prod(r), 3), " CO2 ratio =", round(co2[17] / co2[1], 3), "\n")
  res[[nm]] <- data.frame(year = years, pop = s$pop, gdp = gdp, co2 = co2)
}
for (v in c("pop", "gdp", "co2")) {
  plot(years, res[[1]][[v]], type = "l", lwd = 2, ylim = range(sapply(res, `[[`, v)), xlab = "Year", ylab = v, main = v)
  lines(years, res[[2]][[v]], lwd = 2, lty = 2)
  legend("topleft", names(res), lty = 1:2, bty = "n")
}

Remember

  • Population and labour productivity are inputs, usually taken from an SSP storyline
  • GDP per person drives demand for buildings, transport, industry, food and water
  • The Kaya factors multiply, so their ratios multiply: never add percentages
  • GCAM runs 32 regions in 5-year steps from a 2021 base year to 2100
  • The macro module (KLEM, KLEAM from v9.0) can let energy and policy change GDP

Check yourself

1. In standard GCAM runs, regional population is…

Population is an assumption read from the SSP database, not a model result.

2. Population ×1.2, income per person ×1.5, energy per $ ×0.7, CO₂ per energy ×0.8. CO₂ changes by a factor of…

Multiply the ratios: 1.2 × 1.5 × 0.7 × 0.8 = 1.008.

3. Which equation form does GCAM use for industry and transport service growth?

D_t = D_t−1 (Y ratio)^α (P ratio)^β (N ratio), from the energy demand page.

4. What does the macro module (KLEM/KLEAM) add?

In open-GDP mode energy costs and efficiency feed back into GDP and consumption.

Words and links

New words

Exogenous
Decided outside the model and fed in as an assumption, such as population.
Endogenous
Calculated by the model itself, such as energy prices.
Labour productivity
Output per worker; its growth rate is a key GDP assumption.
Income elasticity
The % change in demand for a 1% change in income per person.
KLEAM
GCAM's v9.0 macro module: capital, labour, energy, agriculture and materials.
