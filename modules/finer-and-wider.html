Research brief

In this module you will

  • Describe what it takes to model a country as its own GCAM region, as GCAM-KSA does

Spoken overview
Read the transcript

Global regions are often too coarse for national policy. There are two ways to go finer. The first is to give the country its own region, as the GCAM-KSA model does for Saudi Arabia, separating it from the Middle East region. That means splitting data for population, economy, energy, land and water, and recalibrating. KAPSARC transport studies with GCAM-KSA report 2060 transport emissions under several scenarios. The second is downscaling: running the regional model and then sharing its results among countries. Proportional downscaling keeps today's shares. Convergence methods, inspired by DSCALE, let countries' intensities move towards the regional average over time, then rescale so the countries still add up to the regional total. To go wider, couple GCAM with other models. GCAM's transport sector works in sales and service, not vehicle fleets, so a vehicle-stock model is needed to talk about how many cars are on the road. National models such as LEAP can take GCAM's prices and demands. The hard part of coupling is convergence: deciding which variables pass between models, and iterating until they agree.

A country of its own: the GCAM-KSA approach

Core GCAM has 32 regions; Saudi Arabia is inside "Middle East", and its transport parameters come from the UC Davis "Middle East" region (energy/mappings/UCD_ctry.csv). GCAM-KSA v1.0 is GCAM 6.0 with Saudi Arabia as a 33rd region, calibrated to 2015 with KAPSARC data. To build a country region yourself:

  1. Add the country to common/iso_GCAM_regID.csv and the region-name table, so gcamdata splits it from its parent region.
  2. Check every mapping that is by country or by UCD region (transport uses UCD_ctry.csv) so the new region gets parameters.
  3. Override region-average values with national data where you have it: fleet, efficiency, load factors, fuel prices, elasticities (KAPSARC used its transport and infrastructure team's data and Saudi literature such as fuel-price elasticity studies).
  4. Re-check calibration: base-year transport energy by fuel must match national balances.

Why this matters

In GCAM-KSA, the version of GCAM in which KAPSARC and PNNL split Saudi Arabia out of the Middle East, Saudi transport CO2 in 2060 ranges from 418 Mt with no policy to 115 Mt under net zero — against 176 Mt in 2015. Even in the net-zero case, around 80 % of new cars are zero- or low-emission, and aviation and shipping dominate what is left. None of these numbers can be read from core GCAM, where Saudi Arabia shares its transport parameters with Iran, Iraq and the rest of the region.

Research note

Access. The GCAM-KSA documentation (KAPSARC, dated 2 June 2026) sits behind a registration form and was not read for this handbook; GCAM-KSA itself is not part of the public gcam-core release. Students without access can (a) work with the Middle East region of core GCAM and state that Saudi values are regional averages, or (b) build a country split as above, which is a substantial project in itself.

Downscaling: from a region to its countries

When building a new region is too expensive, results can be downscaled: a regional pathway is split among countries using country data, with the rule that the countries add back to the region. The simplest rule keeps today's shares; better rules let shares move.

DSCALE (Sferra et al. 2026, Geosci. Model Dev. 19, 3157–3191, doi:10.5194/gmd-19-3157-2026) downscales regional mitigation pathways to 143 countries. Its central idea is convergence: each country starts on its own trend and converges towards the regional pattern by a chosen year. In its simplest form, for a country's share s of a regional variable:

sc(t) = (1 − wt) · sc(t0) + wt · sc*,    wt = min(1, (t − t0) ⁄ (Tconv − t0))(3.11)

This linear blend is a teaching simplification of DSCALE's method, which blends a national-trend projection with the regional IAM pathway, reconciles energy variables by iterated proportional rescaling and treats emissions separately. Read the paper before using the method in research.

The kit's demo goes one step beyond Eq. 3.11: it converges each country's energy intensity (energy per unit of GDP) log-linearly towards the regional intensity, multiplies by each country's own GDP path, and then harmonises — rescales — so the countries add up exactly to the GCAM regional total. On its synthetic six-country region both methods reproduce the regional total to within 10−15 EJ, but they disagree strongly by 2100: the fast-growing Country B gets 0.98 EJ under convergence against 0.75 EJ under fixed shares. The 2050 harmonisation factor of 0.60 shows how far the unharmonised country paths overshoot the region — a number worth reporting.

Worked calculation

Downscale Saudi Arabia's share of Middle East transport energy

  1. Starting share in 2020: 0.50 (illustrative). Long-run target share s* (e.g. from GDP share): 0.42.
  2. Convergence by 2060: in 2050, w = (2050 − 2020) ÷ (2060 − 2020) = 0.75, so s = 0.25 × 0.50 + 0.75 × 0.42 = 0.44.
  3. Convergence by 2100: w = 30 ÷ 80 = 0.375, so s = 0.625 × 0.50 + 0.375 × 0.42 = 0.47.
  4. Multiply by the regional total; repeat for the other countries and rescale so shares sum to 1.

Answer: 0.44 or 0.47 of the region in 2050 — the convergence year is itself an uncertain assumption

Fleets: what GCAM does not count

GCAM tracks transport as service output by technology and vintage of service, not as vehicles. The query "transport service output by tech (new)" is the nearest thing to sales, but there are no vehicle counts, survival curves or second-hand markets. The difference between new sales and the vehicles on the road is large and slow:

Worked calculation

Sales versus stock with a 15-year vehicle life

  1. Battery-car share of new sales follows an s-curve passing 50 % in 2035.
  2. Stock share in 2035 = average of the last 15 years of sales shares = 0.15.
  3. In 2050 sales are 99.5 % battery cars, but the stock is only 88 %.
  4. Fuel use and CO2 follow the stock, not the sales.

Answer: stock lags sales by roughly a decade: 15 % of cars on the road when half of sales are electric

Coupling GCAM with other models

When a question needs detail GCAM does not have — fleet counts, hourly charging load, national policy detail, behaviour by consumer group — the usual answer is a soft link: two models exchange numbers through files, run in turn, until they agree.

Research note

Convergence is the hard part. Decide in advance which variables must agree (for example, new-vintage BEV share within 2 percentage points in every period), the maximum number of iterations, and what you report if they do not converge. Coupled-model papers that skip this step usually show one model's result labelled as both.

Pitfall

Downscaled numbers are not observations. A country pathway from a downscaling method inherits every assumption of the regional run plus those of the method. Never compare it with a national model as if one were "data"; compare both with history and with each other, and report the convergence year.

"""downscale_demo.py - from a GCAM region to countries: two downscaling methods on SYNTHETIC data.

Problem: GCAM reports transport energy for "Middle East", but a ministry asks about one country.
  Method 1, proportional: each country keeps its base-year share of the regional total.
  Method 2, convergence (inspired by DSCALE, Sferra et al. 2026, Geoscientific Model Development 19,
           doi:10.5194/gmd-19-3157-2026): each country's energy intensity (energy per unit of GDP)
           moves from its own base-year value toward the regional intensity, closing the gap
           log-linearly by a convergence year; country GDP follows its own projection; the
           results are then rescaled every year so countries add up exactly to the GCAM regional
           total (harmonisation). DSCALE itself does much more (several convergence criteria,
           sector detail, emissions with an optimisation step); this demo shows only the idea.
Data: six invented countries ("Country A".."Country F") in an invented region; the regional total
is the toy model's Reference passenger-transport final energy (sample_data). Nothing here is real.
Run: python downscale_demo.py   (writes downscale_results.csv and downscale_demo.png)
"""
import math, os
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib
matplotlib.use("Agg")
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

HERE = os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__))
en = pd.read_csv(os.path.join(HERE, "..", "analysis", "sample_data", "transport_final_energy_by_mode_and_fuel.csv"))
REG = en[en.scenario == "Reference"].groupby("Year").value.sum()        # EJ, regional total
YEARS = list(REG.index)

# invented base-year data: GDP (billion $), GDP growth (%/yr), transport energy (EJ) in 2020
C = pd.DataFrame({
    "country": ["Country A", "Country B", "Country C", "Country D", "Country E", "Country F"],
    "gdp0":    [800, 420, 180, 160, 75, 40],
    "growth":  [0.025, 0.030, 0.020, 0.022, 0.035, 0.040],
    "energy0": [0.80, 0.45, 0.25, 0.16, 0.12, 0.07]})
C["energy0"] *= REG[2020] / C.energy0.sum()           # make 2020 add up to the regional total
CONV_YEAR = 2100

rows = []
for y in YEARS:
    t = y - 2020
    gdp = C.gdp0 * (1 + C.growth) ** t
    # method 1: proportional
    prop = REG[y] * C.energy0 / C.energy0.sum()
    # method 2: convergence of energy intensity toward the regional intensity, then harmonise
    ei0 = C.energy0 / C.gdp0
    reg_ei = REG[y] / gdp.sum()
    w = min(1.0, t / (CONV_YEAR - 2020))
    ei = np.exp((1 - w) * np.log(ei0) + w * math.log(reg_ei))
    raw = ei * gdp
    conv = raw * REG[y] / raw.sum()                    # harmonised to the GCAM regional total
    for i, c in enumerate(C.country):
        rows.append(dict(year=y, country=c, gdp=gdp[i], proportional_EJ=prop[i], convergence_EJ=conv[i],
                         convergence_unharmonised_EJ=raw[i]))
df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
df.to_csv(os.path.join(HERE, "downscale_results.csv"), index=False)

chk = df.groupby("year")[["proportional_EJ", "convergence_EJ"]].sum().sub(REG, axis=0).abs().max().max()
print(f"Regional totals reproduced by both methods: max error {chk:.1e} EJ")
show = df[df.year.isin([2020, 2050, 2100])].pivot_table(index="country", columns="year",
                                                       values=["proportional_EJ", "convergence_EJ"]).round(3)
print(show.to_string())
harm = df[df.year == 2050].convergence_EJ.sum() / df[df.year == 2050].convergence_unharmonised_EJ.sum()
print(f"Harmonisation factor in 2050 (GCAM total / sum of unharmonised country values): {harm:.3f}")

fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 4), sharey=True)
for a, col, title in ((ax[0], "proportional_EJ", "Proportional (fixed 2020 shares)"),
                      (ax[1], "convergence_EJ", "Intensity convergence + harmonisation")):
    p = df.pivot(index="year", columns="country", values=col)
    a.stackplot(p.index, p.T.values, labels=p.columns,
                colors=["#0b3954", "#0f8a8a", "#c4502e", "#4f8a2c", "#c27a1a", "#6a56a8"])
    a.set_title(title + " (synthetic)", fontsize=10); a.set_xlabel("year")
ax[0].set_ylabel("passenger transport final energy (EJ)"); ax[1].legend(fontsize=8, loc="upper left")
fig.tight_layout(); fig.savefig(os.path.join(HERE, "downscale_demo.png"), dpi=130)
print("wrote downscale_results.csv and downscale_demo.png")
Downscales the kit's sample transport energy to three countries with both methods and plots them (harmonisation factor in 2050: 0.601).

Research notes

  • Two ways to go finer: a country region (GCAM-KSA) or downscaling regional results
  • Downscaled countries must still add up to the regional total; state the convergence assumption
  • GCAM models sales and service, not fleets: use a stock model for vehicles on the road
  • Coupling is about which variables pass each way and iterating to agreement

Check yourself

1. What is the main advantage of a country region over downscaling?

A separate region is calibrated to national data and can carry national policies.

2. Downscaled country results must…

Harmonisation keeps the sum equal to the regional result.

3. Why add a vehicle-stock model to GCAM?

Fleet turnover lags sales; a stock model turns sales into vehicles on the road.

4. The hard part of soft-linking two models is…

Convergence between the models is the core difficulty.
