Read the transcript

Every integrated assessment model ends with the same chain. Burning fuel and clearing land puts carbon dioxide into the air. That raises its concentration, measured in parts per million. More carbon dioxide traps more heat; scientists call this extra heat forcing. And extra heat warms the planet. The surprising part is what matters most. Carbon dioxide stays in the air for centuries, so warming follows the running total of everything emitted, not this year's emissions alone. A handy rule says that every thousand billion tonnes of carbon dioxide adds about zero point four five degrees of warming. Picture a bathtub. The tap is yearly emissions, about forty billion tonnes. The drain is almost blocked. The rim is the carbon budget: for a fifty percent chance of staying below one point five degrees, about five hundred billion tonnes were left from the start of twenty twenty. At forty a year, that is about twelve and a half years if emissions stay flat. Turning the tap down slows the rise but does not lower the water. Only reaching net zero stops it rising.

Four links from fuel to fever

Every IAM ends with the same chain. Burning fuel and clearing land put CO2 into the air (emissions). That raises its concentration, measured in parts per million (ppm). More CO2 traps more heat: the extra heat is called forcing, in watts per square metre. Extra heat raises the temperature.

CO2 stays in the air for centuries. So what matters is not this year's emissions but the total ever emitted.

Warming follows the running total

Scientists found a remarkably straight line: each extra 1,000 Gt of CO2 adds about the same warming, wherever you are on the line.

extra warming ≈ 0.45 °C × (CO2 emitted ÷ 1,000 Gt)

The 0.45 is a best estimate; the IPCC's likely range is roughly 0.27 to 0.63 °C per 1,000 Gt. Emitting 2,000 Gt more would add about 0.9 °C.

Common trap

"Emissions are falling, so warming is falling." No. While emissions are above zero the bathtub keeps filling, so CO2 and temperature still rise, just more slowly. Only net zero stops the rise.

The carbon budget is a bathtub

Picture a bathtub. The tap is yearly emissions, about 40 Gt of CO2. The drain is almost blocked, because nature removes CO2 only slowly. The rim is the carbon budget: how much more can go in before a temperature limit is crossed.

  • From the start of 2020, about 500 Gt for a 50% chance of 1.5 °C.
  • About 1,150 Gt for a 67% chance of 2 °C.

At 40 Gt a year, 500 Gt lasts 500 ÷ 40 = 12.5 years if emissions stay flat. If emissions fall in a straight line to zero, the total is a triangle, ½ × 40 × T = 500, so T = 25 years.

Where the analogy breaks: a real bath can be bailed out quickly; removing CO2 from the air is slow and costly.

Try it now

Use the rule: if the world emits 800 Gt more, how much extra warming is that? (Answer: 0.45 × 0.8 = 0.36 °C.) And how many years of today's emissions is 800 Gt? (Answer: 800 ÷ 40 = 20 years.)

Check the budget numbers

The budgets and the 0.45 °C figure come from IPCC AR6 Working Group I (2021) and are counted from the start of 2020, so part has already been used. Check them against the report before you teach with them.

Remember

  • Emissions → concentration → forcing → temperature: four links in every IAM.
  • CO2 stays for centuries, so warming follows the running total of emissions.
  • Rule of thumb: about 0.45 °C per 1,000 Gt of CO2 (likely 0.27–0.63).
  • About 500 Gt was left from 2020 for 1.5 °C (50% chance): 12.5 years at 40 Gt a year.
  • Only net zero stops warming from CO2 rising; turning the tap down only slows it.

Words and links

New words

Concentration
How much CO2 is in the air, in parts per million (ppm). About 280 before industry, about 420 today.
Radiative forcing
Extra heat trapped by greenhouse gases, in watts per square metre (W/m²).
Cumulative emissions
The running total of CO2 emitted over time.
TCRE
Transient climate response to cumulative emissions: warming per 1,000 Gt of CO2, about 0.45 °C (likely 0.27–0.63).
Carbon budget
How much more CO2 can be emitted before a temperature limit is crossed, with a stated probability.
Net zero
Emissions released equal emissions removed, so the total in the air stops rising.
