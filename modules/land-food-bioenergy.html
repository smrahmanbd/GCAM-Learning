Spoken overview
Read the transcript

Every hectare of land can only do one job at a time: grow wheat, feed cattle, carry a forest, or grow grass for bioenergy. GCAM divides land among these uses according to how profitable each one is, using a logit, the same family of formulas used for energy technologies but now with a positive exponent on profit. Land is resolved by geographic land units, the overlap of thirty-two regions and river basins, and arranged in a nest, so close substitutes like different crops compete more strongly than cropland and forest. When the energy system wants more bioenergy, the price of energy grass rises, its profit per hectare rises, and land shifts toward it, which pushes up food prices. A carbon price can also be applied to land as a subsidy for holding carbon, which makes forests more profitable and can expand them. In this module you will compute profits and land shares by hand, estimate how many million hectares a bioenergy target needs, and see how yield changes the answer.

What this component represents

The agriculture and land use module (AgLU) decides how land is used, what crops and livestock are produced, and how much carbon land stores. Land is resolved by water-basin-based geographic land units: the overview text says more than 300, its table 396 land subregions (overview.html). Every crop is split into irrigated/rainfed × high/low management, so production can intensify when prices rise.

Profit and land shares

π = (price − cost) × yield
si = (λi πi)ρ / Σj (λj πj)ρ, ai = aparent · si

Worked example (exponent 1, equal scalers): wheat sells at $200/t, costs $120/t, yields 3 t/ha → profit (200 − 120) × 3 = $240/ha. Energy grass at $60/t, cost $30/t, 10 t/ha → (60 − 30) × 10 = $300/ha. Shares: 240 / 540 = 44% wheat, 300 / 540 = 56% grass. At a grass price of $40/t grass gets 29%; at $90/t, 71% (land.html).

A useful mental model

Think of plots on a market street. Each landowner rents to whoever pays most per hectare, but habits, soils and contracts mean some keep their old tenants. The logit is the street's average behaviour.

How much land does bioenergy need?

land (ha) = energy (GJ) / (yield (t/ha) × energy content (GJ/t))

With 18 GJ per tonne: 10 EJ at 10 t/ha needs 10×109 / 180 = 55.6 million ha; 100 EJ needs 555.6 million ha; halve the yield and it doubles to 1,111 million ha. World cropland is roughly 1.5 billion ha, so large bioenergy targets compete seriously with food. GCAM includes default limits: a negative-emissions budget capped at a share of GDP and a biomass externality cost curve (policies.html).

"Bioenergy is carbon-free, so more is always better"

Land conversion can release large stocks of carbon immediately, while regrowth takes decades, and energy crops displace food. GCAM accounts for both through land competition and LUC emissions.

Land carbon and land-use change emissions

Vegetation and soil carbon densities by land unit come from the moirai data system. Land-use change emissions are

Et = At · Dt − At−1 · Dt−1

computed separately for vegetation and soil. Clearing releases carbon immediately; regrowth follows a slow sigmoid with forest mature ages of about 30 to 100 years. A carbon price on land works as a subsidy for holding carbon stock: carbon price × carbon density × discounting and annualisation (land.html).

In GCAM

Query aggregated land allocation and LUC emissions by region; net CO₂ = CO₂ emissions by region + LUC emissions (outputs_emissions.html). Carbon state is set by aglu.CARBON_STATE in gcamdata's constants; maps by basin with rmap::mapGCAMLand.

Calibration and yields

Profit scalers λ are calibrated by inverting the share equation with base-year land shares, then carried forward. Future yields grow by agricultural productivity growth from FAO projections, scaled by SSP (+50% in SSP1/5, −50% in SSP3) (supply_land.html, ssp.html). Numeric land logit exponents are not on the doc pages; they are in the input files.

Docs disagree

Protected land: the policies page says 90% of non-commercial ecosystems by default; the land page describes a suitability × IUCN method. The land page reads as the newer method. Check your release's input files.

# Module 2.6 - Land, food and bioenergy (toy numbers, week 9 example)
profit <- function(price, cost, yield_t) (price - cost) * yield_t
land_shares <- function(p, exponent = 1) { p <- pmax(p, 1e-9); p^exponent / sum(p^exponent) }
for (gp in c(40, 60, 90)) {
  pr <- c(wheat = profit(200, 120, 3), grass = profit(gp, 30, 10))
  cat(sprintf("grass $%d/t: profits %s -> shares %s\n", gp, paste(pr, collapse = "/"), paste(round(land_shares(pr), 3), collapse = "/")))
}
for (x in list(c(10, 10), c(100, 10), c(100, 5)))
  cat(sprintf("%3d EJ at %2d t/ha -> %6.1f million ha\n", x[1], x[2], x[1] * 1e9 / (x[2] * 18) / 1e6))

Remember

  • Land is shared among uses by a logit on profit per hectare, inside a nest
  • Bioenergy and food compete for cropland, so bioenergy demand can raise food prices
  • Land needed = energy ÷ (yield × energy content): yield matters as much as the target
  • Land-use change emits carbon fast and regrowth absorbs it slowly
  • A carbon price on land pays for stored carbon and can expand forests
