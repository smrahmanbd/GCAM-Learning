Research brief

In this module you will

  • Turn a broad topic into a question with a decision, a metric and a comparison
  • Design a scenario matrix with a Reference, levers, policies and sensitivity cases
  • Choose between a carbon price and an emissions constraint for a given question
  • Write an uncertainty plan and a validity argument before running GCAM
Spoken overview
Read the transcript

A good GCAM study answers one clear question. A topic, such as electric vehicles in Saudi Arabia, is not yet a question. A question names a decision, a metric and a comparison: for example, how much would cheaper batteries reduce transport carbon dioxide in 2050 compared with a carbon price that costs the same. Next comes the scenario logic. The Reference run keeps today's policies and assumptions. Levers change one assumption at a time, such as vehicle costs, load factors or the value of time. Policies are added on top, either as a price on carbon, where the model finds the emissions, or as a cap on emissions, where the model finds the price. Then, before you run anything, plan the uncertainty: which inputs are most uncertain, what ranges you will test, and whether you need a small sensitivity analysis or a full ensemble. Finally, write down why anyone should believe your results: calibration, comparison with history, and comparison with other models.

From topic to question

"EVs in Saudi Arabia" is a topic. A research question names a decision, a metric and a comparison:

WeakStrongWhy stronger
What is the future of EVs in KSA?How much do 2060 Saudi transport CO2 emissions differ between a ZEV sales standard and a carbon price that achieves the same 2040 car-emission cut, and at what cost?Decision (instrument choice), metric (CO2, cost), comparison (equal-effort policies)
Can aviation be decarbonised?At what CO2 price does bio-jet with CCS beat DAC offsets for Gulf aviation residuals, and how sensitive is it to biomass supply?A threshold and a sensitivity, both computable
Will rail help freight?How much do Saudi freight emissions fall by 2050 if rail's share-weight doubles, and how much of that survives a freight logit exponent of −4 instead of −2?Separates the policy effect from a structural assumption

Why this matters

Four uncertain inputs with three values each make 81 GCAM runs. Add three policies and you have 243. At two hours a run on four cores, that is about five days of computing — before a single one has failed to solve. Design is the step where you decide which runs answer the question and which only fill a hard drive.

Scenario logic: Reference, levers and policies

Every GCAM study compares runs that differ in a controlled way. Three layers are enough for most transport questions:

  1. Background world: an SSP (population, GDP, the matching transport file transportation_UCD_SSP1/3/5.xml; SSP2 uses CORE) and current policies.
  2. Levers: the assumptions your question is about — BEV cost, share-weight path, load factors, speed of rail, a fuel technology you add.
  3. Policies: none, a carbon price, an emissions constraint (net zero), a standard (ZEV credits, fuel quantity cap).

Hold everything else at Reference. If two things change at once, you cannot say which caused the difference.

Worked calculation

How many runs, and how long?

  1. Full factorial: 3 SSPs × 2 BEV cost levels × 2 share-weight paths × 3 policies = 36 runs.
  2. At about 2 hours per run (machine-dependent; check your own) and 4 runs in parallel: 36 × 2 ÷ 4 = 18 hours.
  3. Add a 3-level freight exponent test only for the central SSP and two policies: + 3 × 2 = 6 runs.
  4. Allow 10 % for failed runs and re-runs: about 46 runs in total.

Answer: ≈ 46 runs, a day of computing — feasible; the 243-run version is not

Policies: price or quantity?

GCAM implements a carbon price (fixedTax), an emissions constraint (constraint, for which the model solves the shadow price), and portfolio standards (credit markets). A price fixes cost and lets emissions follow; a cap fixes emissions and lets the price follow. For comparisons of instruments, match them on one dimension: either equal emissions (then compare cost) or equal price (then compare emissions). Go 2 showed that a cap and a price can be two doors to the same room; in a study you must say which door you used.

Pitfall

Calling an assumption a policy. Setting BEV share-weights to 1 makes BEVs popular; it does not say which policy would make them popular, or what it would cost. If your scenario is "high EV preference", name it as an assumption and, if you can, pair it with a policy (credit standard, subsidy via input-subsidy) whose cost GCAM reports.

Plan the uncertainty before you run

Write a table of 3–6 uncertain inputs, each with a low, central and high value and a source. Choose a design: one-at-a-time (cheap, misses interactions), full factorial (clean, explodes in size), or a Latin-hypercube sample (efficient for many inputs, analysed with regression or scenario discovery). Validity: why should anyone believe it?

  • Calibration check: base-year outputs reproduce IEA energy by fuel (they should, by construction — so this checks the data, not the model).
  • Hindcast: run from 2015 with observed prices and compare 2021–2024 with data you did not calibrate to (e.g. Saudi gasoline demand after the 2016 and 2018 price reforms; IEA EV sales).
  • Benchmark: compare your Reference with other models in the AR6 database or EMF studies for the same region and variables.
  • Face validity: show results to people who know the sector; implausible numbers usually reveal an input error.

Research note

Pre-register your design. Before the first run, write down the scenarios, the uncertain ranges and the figures you will make (the kit's templates/research_proposal.md has the headings). It protects you from running until something looks interesting, and it makes the honest "what the model cannot tell us" section much easier to write.

Try it

  1. Rewrite this into a strong question: "Hydrogen trucks in the Gulf". Name the decision, metric and comparison.
  2. Your design has 2 SSPs × 3 BEV costs × 2 exponents × 3 policies. How many runs? Which factor would you drop first, and why?

# Research proposal template: a GCAM transport study

Fill every section in one to three sentences, then keep this file in the project repository and
update it when the design changes. Two pages maximum.

## 1. Question
- **Policy or science question (one sentence):**
  _e.g. What does a light-duty zero-emission standard achieve in the Middle East by 2050, compared with a carbon price of equal cost?_
- **Who will use the answer, and for what decision?**
- **Why GCAM** (what the question needs that a sector-only model cannot give: fuel-price feedback, electricity and hydrogen supply, land and bioenergy, other regions)?
- **What GCAM cannot answer here** (no vehicle stock or sales in units, no explicit charging infrastructure, generic regional data unless overridden, aviation and shipping liquids not distinct).

## 2. Model and version
- GCAM release: `gcam-v9.1` (or later) — tag and commit hash: ______
- Region(s) studied: ______ (public GCAM 9.1 has 32 energy-economy regions; Saudi Arabia is part of "Middle East" unless you use GCAM-KSA)
- Modifications to the public model (list each file and why). If substantial, name the model "GCAM-<institution>" (GCAM community guidelines).
- Unit conventions checked: transport costs 1975$/vkm and intensities EJ per billion vkm (v8.5+); carbon prices 1990$/tC; CO2 constraints MtC.

## 3. Scenario design (see scenario_design_matrix.csv)
| Dimension | Values | Implemented by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Socioeconomics | e.g. SSP2 (reference), SSP1 and SSP3 as sensitivity | configuration_ssp.xml / batch files |
| Technology | Reference; BEV cost -30%; ... | xml/trn_bev_cost_low.xml ... |
| Behaviour | Reference; load factor +20% ... | xml/trn_loadfactor_up.xml |
| Policy | None; carbon tax; net-zero constraint; ZEV standard | xml/policy_*.xml |

- Number of runs: ______ (dimensions multiply; check run time and disk space).
- Which comparison answers the question (e.g. policy run minus its own reference, same assumptions otherwise)?

## 4. Indicators
- Primary: ______ (e.g. direct transport CO2 2050 in MtCO2; ZEV share of car pkm)
- Secondary: final energy by fuel; service by mode; electricity and hydrogen demand; carbon price; cost of transport services.
- Queries used: `queries/transport_queries.xml` (titles listed in its header).

## 5. Uncertainty plan
- Which inputs are uncertain, and ranges with sources (table).
- Method: one-at-a-time sensitivity / ensemble (n = __) / scenario discovery on the ensemble (analysis/scenario_discovery.py).
- What result would change the policy conclusion?

## 6. Validation and checks
- Base year: compare calibrated 2021 transport energy with IEA balances for the region.
- Recent trend: compare 2025 model years with observed EV sales / fuel use where available.
- Solver: every period solved; log checked for warnings.
- Sanity: decomposition (analysis/decompose_lmdi.py) explains the CO2 change with sensible signs.

## 7. Outputs and timeline
- Figures and tables (list), report sections, code and data release plan (repository + DOI).
- Weeks 1–2 design and setup · 3–4 runs · 5 analysis · 6 write-up and presentation.
Question, motivation, scenarios, uncertainty plan, validity and expected outputs.

Stress-test it

Generate an ensemble over three uncertain inputs, define 'high emissions', and find a simple rule that explains the high runs.

Predict first: Of GDP growth, clean-technology cost decline and efficiency gain, which do you expect to explain most high-emission runs?

Research notes

  • A question names a decision, a metric and a comparison
  • Change one lever at a time against a fully described Reference
  • A price fixes cost and finds emissions; a cap fixes emissions and finds the price
  • Plan inputs, ranges and method for uncertainty before the first run
  • State why results are credible: calibration, history and other models

New words

Reference scenario
The counterfactual run without the policy or change being studied.
Lever
A single assumption you change to test its effect.
Scenario matrix
The planned set of runs crossing levers, policies and sensitivity cases.
Shadow price
The carbon price GCAM finds when an emissions constraint binds.
Hindcast
Running a model over a past period to compare with what actually happened.
