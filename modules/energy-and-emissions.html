Read the transcript

People do not want energy for its own sake. They want services: light, warm homes, travel, cold drinks, steel. Energy travels a chain to get there. It starts as primary energy, the coal, oil, gas, sunlight or wind that nature provides. Power plants and refineries convert it into final energy, such as electricity and petrol, which reaches homes and factories. Each step loses some. Of every hundred units of coal in a typical power plant, only about thirty six reach homes as electricity. So saving one unit at home saves almost three at the mine. To see what drives emissions, split them into four factors that multiply. This is the Kaya identity. Carbon dioxide equals people, times income per person, times energy per dollar, times carbon dioxide per unit of energy. The first two are about society. The last two are about technology, and that is where integrated assessment models spend most of their detail. Because the factors multiply, you cannot add their changes. Twenty percent more people, fifty percent more income, thirty percent less energy per dollar and twenty percent less carbon per unit of energy gives almost no change in emissions, not a twenty percent rise.

People want services, not energy

Nobody wants a kilowatt-hour for its own sake. People want services: light, warm homes, travel, cold drinks, steel. Energy travels a chain to get there: primary energy is what nature provides (coal, oil, gas, sunlight, wind); conversion in power plants and refineries turns it into final energy (electricity, petrol); final energy becomes services.

Process-based IAMs follow the whole chain and choose technologies at each step by cost. That is why they can compare supply options, such as solar, with demand options, such as better buildings.

Every step loses some

Illustrative chain: 100 units of coal enter a 40%-efficient power plant, leaving 100 × 0.40 = 40 units of electricity. The grid loses 10%, so 40 × 0.90 = 36 units reach homes. About two thirds are lost on the way.

Turn it around: saving 1 unit at home saves 100 ÷ 36 ≈ 2.8 units of coal at the start of the chain.

A useful mental model

Think of a leaky pipeline from the mine to your phone. Fixing a leak near the end saves the most water, because everything upstream was pumped for it.

The Kaya identity: four drivers

To see what drives emissions, split them into four factors that multiply, named after the energy economist Yoichi Kaya:

CO2 = people × (GDP ÷ people) × (energy ÷ GDP) × (CO2 ÷ energy)

It is not a theory: the units cancel, so it is always true. Its value is as a way of sorting data. The first two factors are about society; the last two are about technology.

Multiply, never add

Avalon is an invented country. Over 30 years its population grows 20%, income per person 50%, energy per dollar falls 30% and CO2 per unit of energy falls 20%.

Multiply the ratios: 1.2 × 1.5 × 0.7 × 0.8 = 1.008. CO2 rises by under 1%: growth in people and income is almost exactly cancelled by cleaner, more efficient technology. Adding the percentages (+20 + 50 − 30 − 20 = +20%) gives the wrong answer.

Common trap

Adding percentage changes. The Kaya factors multiply, so combine their ratios by multiplying: +20% and −20% do not cancel (1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96).

Try it now

A country's population doubles and its income per person halves. Energy per dollar and CO2 per unit of energy stay the same. What happens to its CO2? (Answer: 2 × 0.5 = 1, no change.)

Remember

  • People want energy services; energy flows from primary to final energy to services.
  • Every conversion step loses energy; in the example only 36 of 100 units reach homes.
  • Kaya: CO2 = people × income per person × energy per $ × CO2 per energy.
  • Society sets the first two factors; technology sets the last two.
  • The factors multiply, so combine ratios by multiplying, not by adding percentages.

Check yourself

1. Which is an energy service?

A service is what people actually want. Electricity is final energy; oil and gas are primary energy.

2. 100 units of coal, a 40%-efficient plant and 10% grid losses deliver how many units to homes?

100 × 0.40 = 40, then 40 × 0.90 = 36.

3. People +10%, income per person +20%, energy per $ −50%, CO2 per energy −10%. CO2 changes by a factor of:

Multiply the ratios: 1.1 × 1.2 × 0.5 × 0.9 = 0.594, about a 41% fall.

4. Which two Kaya factors are mainly about technology?

Efficiency sets energy per dollar; the fuel mix sets CO2 per unit of energy.

5. Avalon's factors change by +20%, +50%, −30% and −20%. Its CO2:

1.2 × 1.5 × 0.7 × 0.8 = 1.008, a rise of about 0.8%. Adding percentages gives the wrong +20%.

Words and links

New words

Primary energy
Energy as nature provides it: coal, oil, gas, uranium, sunlight, wind, biomass.
Final energy
Energy delivered to users, such as electricity, petrol and heat.
Energy service
What people actually want from energy: light, warmth, travel, cooling, materials.
Kaya identity
CO2 = population × GDP per person × energy per GDP × CO2 per energy; always true because the units cancel.
Energy intensity
Energy used per dollar of economic output (energy ÷ GDP).
Carbon intensity
CO2 released per unit of energy (CO2 ÷ energy).
