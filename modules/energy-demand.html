Spoken overview
Read the transcript

Nobody wants a gigajoule for its own sake. People want warm and cool rooms, light, steel for buildings, and trips to work and school. GCAM therefore models demand for energy services first, and only then the fuels needed to deliver them. Service demand grows with income and falls when services get more expensive, through income and price elasticities. Buildings demand depends on floor space, which rises with income and then levels off, and on heating and cooling degree days. Industry is split into sectors such as steel, cement, chemicals and fertiliser. Transport is special because time matters. A trip by bus is cheap in money but slow, while a car or a train is faster. GCAM adds the value of time to the money cost of each mode, using the wage, so as people get richer they move toward faster modes. In this module you will compute service demand, then mode shares for walking, bus, rail and car at three wage levels, and watch the car share grow from three percent to sixty-two percent.

Services, not fuels

GCAM's demand sectors produce energy services (floorspace heated and cooled, industrial output, passenger-km and tonne-km), then choose technologies and fuels to supply them (demand_energy.html). Service demand changes with income and price:

Dt = Dt−1 · (Yt/Yt−1)α · (Pt/Pt−1)β · (Nt/Nt−1)

With α = 0.6 and β = −0.3, doubling income raises passenger-km from 100 to 151.6; if travel also becomes 50% dearer it is 134.2.

A useful mental model

A restaurant plans meals before it orders gas. GCAM does the same: meals = services, gas = fuels. Change the menu (services) or the stove (technology) and the gas bill changes.

Buildings

Residential demand is split into 10 income deciles (since v7.1). Floorspace per person follows a Gompertz curve in income and population density; services per m² saturate; heating and cooling scale with degree-days and shell efficiency (demand_energy.html). Degree-days link buildings to climate; the helios tool computes them from gridded data. Parameter values for these curves are not given on the doc pages.

Industry

Industry includes iron and steel (BOF, EAF with scrap, EAF with DRI), chemicals, aluminium, cement, fertiliser, food processing and other industry, plus construction, mining and agricultural energy. Fertiliser demand is the sum of crop production × fertiliser coefficient: a direct link from farms to the energy system (demand_energy.html).

Transport: money plus time

Each vehicle technology has a cost per passenger-km or tonne-km:

Pj = (Pfuel · Ij + Nj) / Lj

with fuel intensity I, non-fuel cost per vehicle-km N and load factor L. Passenger modes then add the value of time:

Pmode = Σ αj Pj + W · V / S

W is wage per hour (GDP per person ÷ working hours), V a time-value multiplier, S speed. In the kit toy (value of time = half the wage), car share rises from 3% at $2/h to 22.5% at $10/h and 61.7% at $40/h.

In GCAM

Query final energy consumption by sector and fuel and transport service output by mode (outputs_quantity.html). Transport inputs are built by gcamdata from the UCD transportation database (UCD_trn_data_SSP*.csv) (ssp.html). Note: transport units changed in v8.5 (billion pkm, 1975$); Go 3.2 covers the details.

"Richer people always drive more"

Not necessarily: they choose faster modes. Where high-speed rail is fast and frequent, rising wages can favour rail. The value of time rewards speed, not cars as such.

# Module 2.5 - Energy demand: service demand and mode choice with the cost of time (toy numbers)
service_demand <- function(base, income_ratio, price_ratio, inc_el = 0.6, price_el = -0.3)
  base * income_ratio^inc_el * price_ratio^price_el
cat("pkm if income doubles:", round(service_demand(100, 2, 1), 1), "; and 50% dearer:", round(service_demand(100, 2, 1.5), 1), "\n")
modes <- data.frame(mode = c("walk", "bus", "rail", "car"), money = c(0, 0.05, 0.12, 0.30), speed = c(5, 15, 30, 60))
for (wage in c(2, 10, 40)) {
  gc <- modes$money + 0.5 * wage / modes$speed       # value of time = half the wage
  sh <- gc^-3 / sum(gc^-3)
  cat(sprintf("wage %2d $/h: ", wage)); print(setNames(round(sh, 3), modes$mode))
}

Remember

  • GCAM models energy services first, then the fuels that supply them
  • Service demand rises with income (α) and falls with price (β)
  • Buildings use floorspace and degree-days; industry is split into subsectors
  • Passenger modes compete on money cost plus wage-based time cost
  • The electricity price reaches heat pumps, EVs and electric furnaces through technology choice

Words and links

New words

Energy service
The useful outcome of energy use: warmth, light, motion, materials.
Price elasticity
The % change in demand for a 1% change in its price.
Degree-days
A measure of how much heating or cooling a climate needs.
Value of time
The money value travellers place on time spent travelling, tied to the wage.
Generalised cost
Money cost plus the money value of travel time.
