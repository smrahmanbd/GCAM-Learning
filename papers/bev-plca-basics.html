Click a car, then a driving mode. The moving dashes show which way energy flows. Watch for regenerative braking, when the motor runs backwards as a generator and puts energy back into the battery.

2Animation: where does the energy go?

Put 100 units of energy into each car (in the tank, the battery or the hydrogen tank) and watch how much actually turns the wheels. Rough typical values, not from the paper.

Electric motors waste little energy. Engines lose most of it as heat. That is why a BEV needs only about 19–29 kWh per 100 km in the paper, roughly a third of the energy in the diesel an engine would burn. A fuel cell sits in between. But efficiency is not the whole story: what matters for the climate is also how dirty the energy was to make. That is the next two sections.

3Interactive: make your own electricity grid

A BEV is only as clean as the electricity it charges on. Move the sliders to build a grid (the shares are rescaled to 100%) and watch the BEV's use-phase emissions change. The hybrid line stays put, because it does not use the grid.

Life-cycle emission factors per kWh generated, rounded medians from IPCC AR5 (coal 820, gas 490, solar 48, hydro 24, nuclear 12, wind 11 g CO₂e/kWh), plus about 8% grid losses. BEV: 0.20 kWh/km at the plug. HEV: 5.5 L diesel per 100 km × (2.68 exhaust + 0.55 supply) kg CO₂e/L.

4Interactive: make your own hydrogen

Today most hydrogen comes from natural gas. Choose a mix of production routes. Grid electrolysis uses the grid you built above: about 52 kWh of electricity per kg of H₂, so a dirty grid gives dirty hydrogen.

Illustrative factors: steam methane reforming (SMR) 11, coal gasification 20, renewable electrolysis 1, BECCS −15 kg CO₂e per kg H₂. FCEV: 1.0 kg H₂ per 100 km. In the paper's 1.5 °C scenario, TIAM-UCL builds a lot of BECCS hydrogen. That is why FCEVs come out best there.

6Animation: what a life cycle assessment counts

An LCA adds up the greenhouse gases from every stage of a product's life, cradle to grave:

  1. Raw materials and manufacturing. Steel, aluminium, battery metals, factories.
  2. Use. Making the energy (well-to-tank), plus anything coming out of the tailpipe (tank-to-wheel).
  3. End of life. Dismantling and recycling.

Different gases are converted to CO₂-equivalents (CO₂e) using their 100-year global warming potential (IPCC 2021). The total is divided by the lifetime distance, giving the functional unit: g CO₂e per km.

The trap the paper fixes

A typical LCA uses 2025 data for all stages. But the use stage happens later, when energy will be cleaner. A prospective, time-adjusted LCA gives each year of driving its own energy mix.

7Scenarios from an integrated assessment model

An integrated assessment model (IAM) represents the world's energy system, economy and emissions. It asks: if the world aims for X °C, which power plants, fuels and hydrogen plants get built, where and when? The paper uses TIAM-UCL, which picks the cheapest way to meet energy demand within a climate limit, across 16 regions.
