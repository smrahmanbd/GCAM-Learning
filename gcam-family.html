Climate

Hector

Turns emissions into concentrations, forcing and temperature, inside GCAM

Use the arrows or the ← → keys. Every deck has the same seven stops: one sentence, steps, a 3D view, inputs and outputs, where it plugs in, try it, quiz.

In one sentence

Hector is a small, fast climate model: it takes the world's emissions each year and returns how much CO2 is in the air, how much extra heat the planet traps, and how warm it gets.

  • It is 'reduced complexity': a few hundred equations instead of a supercomputer model, so it runs in under a second.
  • It is built from boxes of carbon (air, plants, soil, ocean layers) with flows between them.
  • GCAM calls Hector every period, so climate is part of the model, not a separate step afterwards.

How it works

How Hector works, step by step

  1. Emissions arriveGCAM hands over CO2, methane, nitrous oxide, aerosols and other gases for the period.
  2. Carbon moves between boxesSome CO2 stays in the atmosphere; plants and soils take some; the ocean surface absorbs some and slowly passes it to deeper layers.
  3. Concentration is read offThe carbon left in the atmosphere box gives the CO2 concentration in parts per million.
  4. Forcing is computedEach gas and aerosol adds or subtracts radiative forcing, the extra energy trapped per square metre.
  5. Temperature respondsA simple energy-balance model (DOECLIM) warms the surface and mixes heat into the ocean, so warming lags forcing by decades.
  6. Feedbacks close the loopWarmer soils release carbon faster and the warmer ocean absorbs less, so next year's boxes shift.

In 3D

The carbon boxes in 3D

The 3D view needs JavaScript and WebGL.

Carbon (the moving dots) flows from the atmosphere into vegetation, detritus and soil on land, and into the surface, intermediate and deep ocean. The thermometer rises as the atmosphere box fills.

  • The atmosphere box grows while emissions run; the ocean boxes catch up slowly.
  • Deep ocean is the biggest box but the slowest to fill: that is why warming is so persistent.
  • Drag to orbit; scroll to zoom.

Inputs and outputs

What goes in, what comes out

InputsYearly emissions of CO2 (fossil and land-use), CH4, N2O, halocarbons, SO2, black and organic carbon, ozone precursors; a handful of parameters such as climate sensitivity.
OutputsCO2 concentration (ppm), radiative forcing (W/m2), global mean temperature change (°C), ocean heat and pH, carbon fluxes by box.
TimeAnnual steps, from 1745 to 2300 if you want; GCAM uses the values at its five-year periods.
SizeA few hundred equations in C++ with an R package wrapper; a run takes well under a second.
VersionHector v3 (2024) added a permafrost module and new ocean chemistry; check which version your GCAM release carries.

Where it plugs in

Where it plugs into GCAM

GCAM core
Goes in

Emissions by gas, summed over all regions and sectors, every period.

Comes back

CO2 concentration, forcing and temperature; used for climate-target scenarios (a forcing or temperature cap) and reported in results.

How often

Every five-year period, inside the solver loop, so a cap on forcing can change the carbon price in the same run.

Note. Before GCAM 4.3 the climate step was MAGICC; Hector replaced it so that the whole model is open source.

Hydrology

Xanthos

Turns rain and temperature on a world grid into river runoff for GCAM's 235 water basins

Use the arrows or the ← → keys. Every deck has the same seven stops: one sentence, steps, a 3D view, inputs and outputs, where it plugs in, try it, quiz.

In one sentence

Xanthos is a global hydrology model: for every half-degree cell of land it works out how much rain becomes runoff, then routes that water down rivers and adds it up by basin.

  • It answers one question for GCAM: how much renewable water is there in each basin, each year, under a given climate?
  • It is modular: you choose a method for evaporation, a method for runoff and a method for routing.
  • It runs outside GCAM: its basin runoff becomes GCAM's renewable water supply input.

How it works

How Xanthos works, step by step

  1. Climate comes inMonthly precipitation and temperature for each 0.5° cell (about 67,000 land cells), from observations or a climate model.
  2. Potential evapotranspirationHow much water the air could take back if it were available, from temperature and sunshine (Hargreaves, Penman-Monteith or Thornthwaite).
  3. Soil water balanceA bucket per cell fills with rain and drains by evaporation, so runoff appears when the bucket overflows (the ABCD model, for example).
  4. Routing down the riverRunoff from each cell flows to its downstream neighbour along a river network, with a delay, until it reaches the basin outlet.
  5. Sum by basinFlows are totalled for the 235 basins GCAM uses, giving renewable water per basin per year.
  6. Hand to GCAMMinus environmental flows, plus reservoir storage, this becomes the top tier of GCAM's water supply curve.

In 3D

Rain to river in 3D

The 3D view needs JavaScript and WebGL.

Rain falls on a terrain of grid cells; each drop sinks into the soil bucket and, when the bucket is full, runs downhill to the outlet, where the basin total accumulates.

  • Cells on high ground drain first; the valley collects.
  • The counter at the outlet is what GCAM receives for the basin.
  • Drag to orbit; scroll to zoom.

Inputs and outputs

What goes in, what comes out

InputsGridded monthly precipitation, temperature, and for some methods humidity, wind and radiation; a flow-direction map; soil and basin masks.
ModulesPotential evapotranspiration (several methods); runoff (ABCD water balance or GWAM); routing (a modified river transport model); optional drought and hydropower modules.
OutputsRunoff and streamflow per cell and per basin, monthly and yearly; basin totals formatted for GCAM.
Resolution0.5° cells, monthly steps, any period your climate data cover.
CodeOpen-source Python (JGCRI/xanthos); v1 described by Li et al. (2017), v2 by Vernon et al. (2019).

Where it plugs in

Where it plugs into GCAM

GCAM core
Goes in

Nothing from GCAM: Xanthos is driven by climate data chosen for the scenario.

Comes back

Renewable water by basin, which with environmental flow requirements and reservoir storage sets the accessible-water tier of GCAM's supply curve; groundwater and desalination sit above it.

How often

Run once per climate scenario, before GCAM; a different climate means a different water input file.

Note. Because Xanthos runs first, GCAM's water scarcity depends on the climate you feed Xanthos, not on the warming Hector computes in the same run (no feedback by default).

Water downscaling

Tethys

Spreads GCAM's water withdrawals by region and sector onto a monthly world grid

Use the arrows or the ← → keys. Every deck has the same seven stops: one sentence, steps, a 3D view, inputs and outputs, where it plugs in, try it, quiz.

In one sentence

Tethys takes GCAM's answer to 'how much water does each region and sector withdraw in 2050?' and spreads it over a map and over the months of the year.

  • GCAM reports water by region, basin and sector (irrigation, livestock, households, electricity, manufacturing, mining), not by place.
  • Tethys uses proxies (where people live, where crops are irrigated, where power plants stand) to decide where the water is used.
  • The result is a gridded, monthly water-use map that hydrology and power-grid models can read.

How it works

How Tethys works, step by step

  1. Read GCAM withdrawalsWater withdrawn per region, basin and sector for each GCAM period.
  2. Pick a proxy per sectorPopulation for households; irrigated area by crop for irrigation; livestock density; power plant locations for cooling; and so on.
  3. Spread over the gridEach cell gets a share of the sector's water in proportion to its proxy, inside the right region and basin.
  4. Spread over the monthsIrrigation follows the growing season, household use follows temperature, electricity follows demand; other sectors are flat.
  5. Interpolate between periodsGCAM steps are five years; Tethys fills the years between.
  6. Write the mapMonthly water withdrawal per 0.5° cell (finer in newer versions), by sector.

In 3D

From one number to a map in 3D

The 3D view needs JavaScript and WebGL.

A region's water total (the tall block) breaks into grid cells whose heights follow the proxy; then the year turns and the irrigation cells pulse with the seasons.

  • The sum of the small columns always equals the big block: downscaling conserves the total.
  • Only some cells change with the month; those are irrigation and cooling water.

Inputs and outputs

What goes in, what comes out

InputsGCAM water withdrawal output; gridded population, irrigated crop area, livestock, power plant data; monthly temperature and irrigation profiles.
OutputsGridded water withdrawals by sector, monthly, for every year of the scenario.
Resolution0.5° by default; Tethys v2 can run finer and on other region sets.
CodeOpen-source Python (JGCRI/tethys); Li et al. (2018) for v1, Khan et al. (2023) for v2.
Why it mattersWater stress is local and seasonal; a regional annual number hides both.

Where it plugs in

Where it plugs into GCAM

GCAM core
Goes in

GCAM's water withdrawal results by region, basin and sector.

Comes back

Nothing back to GCAM by default; the maps go to other models (hydrology, grids, impact studies) and to policy maps.

How often

After a GCAM run, as post-processing.

Note. Tethys is a one-way downscaler. If a study needs the local map to change GCAM, the loop must be built by hand.

Land downscaling

Demeter

Spreads GCAM's land allocation by region and basin onto a map of grid cells

Use the arrows or the ← → keys. Every deck has the same seven stops: one sentence, steps, a 3D view, inputs and outputs, where it plugs in, try it, quiz.

In one sentence

Demeter takes GCAM's land results ('this region-basin has 20% more cropland in 2050') and decides which grid cells change, so that the result is a land-cover map.

  • GCAM allocates land by profit within about 300 land units; it does not know where inside a unit a field is.
  • Demeter starts from a real base-year map and changes as little as possible, cell by cell, to match GCAM's new totals.
  • Earth-system and hydrology models need maps, not totals; Demeter is the bridge.

How it works

How Demeter works, step by step

  1. Start from a mapA base-year land-cover map at the target resolution, with GCAM's land types mapped onto it.
  2. Read GCAM's changeFor each region-basin and land type: how much area must be gained or lost this period.
  3. Rank cells by suitabilityConstraints such as soil quality, slope and nearness to existing land of the same type give each cell a score.
  4. Intensify firstWhere a land type already exists in a cell, raise its share before touching new cells.
  5. Then expandIf more area is still needed, convert the best-scoring neighbouring cells, following a transition priority (crops take pasture before forest, for example).
  6. Write the mapA land-cover map for each GCAM period, with fractions of each type in each cell.

In 3D

Intensify, then expand, in 3D

The 3D view needs JavaScript and WebGL.

A field of cells: existing cropland cells rise first (intensification); when they are full, the best neighbouring cells turn to cropland (expansion), while forest cells shrink to balance.

  • Expansion spreads outward from existing cropland: the kernel-density rule.
  • The total area added equals the total GCAM asked for.

Inputs and outputs

What goes in, what comes out

InputsGCAM land allocation output; a base-year land-cover map; spatial constraint maps (soil, slope, protected areas); a transition priority table; a type mapping.
OutputsFractional land cover by type for each cell and GCAM period.
ResolutionConfigurable; published runs use 0.05° (about 5 km) and 0.5°.
CodeOpen-source Python (JGCRI/demeter); Vernon et al. (2018); a global dataset by Chen et al. (2020).
Why it mattersWhere forest is lost matters for carbon, water and biodiversity; GCAM alone cannot say where.

Where it plugs in

Where it plugs into GCAM

GCAM core
Goes in

GCAM's land allocation by region, basin and land type, every period.

Comes back

Nothing back by default; maps go to Earth-system models, hydrology (including Xanthos-type models), and impact studies.

How often

After a GCAM run.

Note. Demeter and Tethys are the two standard downscalers; both conserve GCAM's totals and both are one-way.

Land data

moirai

Builds the land, crop and carbon data that GCAM's land module starts from

Use the arrows or the ← → keys. Every deck has the same seven stops: one sentence, steps, a 3D view, inputs and outputs, where it plugs in, try it, quiz.

In one sentence

moirai is the Land Data System: it cuts the world into land units (country × water basin), and for each unit counts land area by type, crop production and harvested area, and the carbon stored in vegetation and soil.

  • GCAM's land module needs a consistent base year: how much land of each kind, growing what, holding how much carbon.
  • moirai lines up maps and tables that were never designed to agree (land-use history, crop maps, FAO statistics, carbon maps) and makes them agree.
  • Its outputs feed gcamdata, which turns them into GCAM's land input files.

How it works

How moirai works, step by step

  1. Gather the layersHistorical land use (HYDE), crop harvested area and yields by grid cell (SAGE), FAO country statistics, potential vegetation, and vegetation and soil carbon maps.
  2. Define land unitsOverlay countries with the 235 water basins to get geographic land units (GLUs).
  3. Cut every layer by unitEach map is sliced by the unit boundaries and summed.
  4. Reconcile with FAOGrid-based crop totals are scaled so that each country's sum matches FAO production and area.
  5. Attach carbonVegetation and soil carbon per hectare by land type and unit, so land-use change has an emission.
  6. Write the tablesLand area, crop production, harvested area and carbon by unit, year and type, for gcamdata.

In 3D

Overlaying the layers in 3D

The 3D view needs JavaScript and WebGL.

Map layers (land use, crops, carbon, basins, countries) stack and are stamped by the land-unit boundaries; the stamped pieces drop into a table.

  • The basin and country layers cut the others: that intersection is a land unit.
  • Every cell ends up in exactly one unit, so nothing is counted twice.

Inputs and outputs

What goes in, what comes out

InputsHYDE land-use history, SAGE/Monfreda crop maps, FAO production and area, MIRCA irrigation, potential vegetation, soil and vegetation carbon, protected-area and basin maps.
OutputsBy land unit: land area by type and year, crop production and harvested area by crop and irrigation, carbon densities; also mapping files.
UnitsCountry × basin; with GCAM's 32 regions this gives the 300-plus land units the land module uses.
CodeOpen-source C with R diagnostics (JGCRI/moirai); Di Vittorio et al. (2016) for v1, v3 in 2020.
Why it mattersEvery land result in GCAM rests on this base year; a mistake here is a mistake everywhere.

Where it plugs in

Where it plugs into GCAM

GCAM core
Goes in

Nothing from GCAM; raw global datasets.

Comes back

Land and carbon tables consumed by gcamdata to build the land-use XML inputs.

How often

Rebuilt when the base year or the source datasets change, not every run.

Note. If you add a new region (as GCAM-KSA did), the land units for that country already exist in moirai; what changes is the region mapping in gcamdata.

Land allocation

The land module

Shares each land unit among crops, pasture, forest and other covers by profit

Use the arrows or the ← → keys. Every deck has the same seven stops: one sentence, steps, a 3D view, inputs and outputs, where it plugs in, try it, quiz.

In one sentence

GCAM's land module decides, in every land unit and every period, how much land goes to each use: it lets uses compete on expected profit, with a logit rule so that the most profitable use wins more land, not all of it.

  • Profit per hectare = price × yield − cost; it rises when food or bioenergy prices rise.
  • The choice is nested: first broad groups (crops versus forest versus grass), then options within a group (which crop, irrigated or rain-fed, fertilised or not).
  • Land-use change emits or stores carbon using the densities from moirai, so land is part of the climate answer.

How it works

How the land module works, step by step

  1. Prices arriveCrop, livestock, forest and bioenergy prices from the markets GCAM is solving.
  2. Profit per useEach land use in each unit gets an expected profit per hectare from price, yield and cost.
  3. Shares by logitWithin each nest, share of use i ∝ share-weight × profitλ; λ sets how sharply land follows profit.
  4. Nests multiplyA crop's share of the unit = its share within the crop nest × the crop nest's share of the unit.
  5. Supply to marketsArea × yield gives crop supply, which the market balances against demand; prices adjust and the loop repeats until all markets clear.
  6. Carbon accountingArea that changed type releases or stores carbon over time, reported as land-use change emissions.

In 3D

A land nest in 3D

The 3D view needs JavaScript and WebGL.

A land unit (the slab) is divided among uses; a rising crop price lifts the profit bar of cropland and its share of the slab grows at the expense of the others, smoothly, never all at once.

  • The shares always sum to the whole slab.
  • A higher λ would make the response sharper; GCAM uses modest values so land changes gradually.

Inputs and outputs

What goes in, what comes out

InputsBase-year land areas, yields and carbon densities (from moirai via gcamdata); yield growth assumptions; logit exponents and calibrated share weights; prices from the solver.
OutputsLand area by type, unit and period; crop and forest supply; land-use change emissions; bioenergy supply.
StructureAbout 300 land units (region × basin); nests for arable versus non-arable, crops by water and fertiliser management, managed and unmanaged forest, pasture, grass, shrub, urban.
Stand-alone versiongcamland, an R package that reproduces the land allocation outside GCAM for testing and calibration.
Watch forShare weights carry calibration judgement; a land result driven by them is an assumption.

Where it plugs in

Where it plugs into GCAM

GCAM core
Goes in

Prices for crops, livestock, forestry and bioenergy from the markets; population and income through food demand.

Comes back

Supplies of crops, forest products and bioenergy; land-use change emissions to Hector; water demand for irrigation to the water markets.

How often

Every period, inside the market solution.

Note. Bioenergy links land to energy: a carbon price makes biomass valuable, land shifts toward it, and food prices feel it.

In the land logit, what happens to a use whose profit is well below the others?

The logit never gives zero; a less profitable use keeps a small share, which is how GCAM represents the many reasons land is not all put to its single best use.

Drivers and markets

The economy in GCAM

Population, income and prices drive demand; markets clear; an optional macro module feeds energy costs back to GDP

Use the arrows or the ← → keys. Every deck has the same seven stops: one sentence, steps, a 3D view, inputs and outputs, where it plugs in, try it, quiz.

In one sentence

GCAM is not a whole-economy model. Population and income are given to it (from the Shared Socioeconomic Pathways); it works out demand for energy, food and water, and finds the prices at which every market balances.

  • Demand grows with income and shrinks with price, through elasticities.
  • Choices between options (fuels, vehicles, power plants, land uses) follow the logit rule on cost.
  • A newer, optional macro module computes GDP from capital, labour, energy and materials, so expensive energy can slow growth.

How it works

How the economy works in GCAM, step by step

  1. DriversPopulation and labour productivity (hence GDP per person) by region and period, from the SSP scenarios.
  2. DemandEach service (travel, heating, food) scales with income and responds to its price.
  3. ChoiceWithin a service, technologies share the market by logit on cost: cheaper wins more.
  4. SupplyResources have graded supply curves: the more you take, the dearer the next unit.
  5. ClearingA solver (Broyden's method) nudges a few hundred prices until supply equals demand everywhere in the period.
  6. Feedback (optional)With the macro module, the cost of energy changes output and investment, so GDP in the next period moves.

In 3D

Markets as scales in 3D

The 3D view needs JavaScript and WebGL.

Each market is a balance: supply on one pan, demand on the other. The solver turns the price dials until every balance is level; when one price moves, others tip, so they must be solved together.

  • Watch a tip in one market propagate to its neighbours before all settle.
  • With the macro loop on, the GDP bar at the back responds to the energy price.

Inputs and outputs

What goes in, what comes out

InputsPopulation and GDP (or labour productivity) by region; income and price elasticities; technology costs and efficiencies; resource supply curves; share weights.
OutputsPrices of every market (energy carriers, crops, water, carbon); demand and supply quantities; with the macro module, GDP and investment.
SolverBroyden's method on log prices; periods solved one after another with no foresight.
Macro moduleKLEM production (capital, labour, energy, materials), extended to KLEAM in v9; off by default in older releases.
Watch forGDP is mostly an input: a carbon price in standard GCAM does not lower GDP unless the macro module is on.

Where it plugs in

Where it plugs into GCAM

GCAM core
Goes in

SSP population and income; everything else is inside the core.

Comes back

Prices and quantities to every sector; emissions to Hector; land and water prices to their modules.

How often

Every period; the market solution is GCAM's main loop.

Note. Compared with REMIND or MESSAGE-MACRO, GCAM's economy is lighter: no intertemporal optimisation, and growth is given unless the macro module is used.

Data system

gcamdata

Turns raw statistics into the XML input files GCAM reads

Use the arrows or the ← → keys. Every deck has the same seven stops: one sentence, steps, a 3D view, inputs and outputs, where it plugs in, try it, quiz.

In one sentence

gcamdata is an R package that builds GCAM's inputs: it reads hundreds of raw files (energy balances, FAO, SSPs, moirai, technology costs) and runs them through a chain of small functions until the result is the XML files the C++ core loads.

  • Every number in GCAM's base year can be traced back through gcamdata to a source file.
  • The chain is made of 'chunks', each with declared inputs and outputs, so changing one file reruns only what depends on it.
  • Regional versions (GCAM-USA, GCAM-KSA) are made largely by adding and changing chunks and mapping files.

How it works

How gcamdata works, step by step

  1. Raw inputsIEA energy balances, FAO food and land statistics, SSP population and GDP, moirai land tables, technology cost assumptions, mapping files.
  2. Level 0 and 1Read and clean; convert units; aggregate to GCAM regions and sectors.
  3. Level 2Build the structures GCAM expects: sectors, subsectors, technologies, share weights, elasticities, resource curves.
  4. CalibrationSet share weights and efficiencies so the base year reproduces the data.
  5. XMLWrite each table as XML in GCAM's schema; the configuration file lists which XML files a run loads.
  6. RunThe C++ core reads the XML, solves period by period, and writes an output database that you query.

In 3D

The pipeline in 3D

The 3D view needs JavaScript and WebGL.

Data cubes travel along a conveyor through chunk stations, each transforming them (colour and shape change), until they are stamped into XML files at the end and loaded into the GCAM core.

  • A cube that fails a check stops the line: that is a chunk error.
  • Add-on XML (the side lane) joins at the end and overrides values from the main files.

Inputs and outputs

What goes in, what comes out

InputsRaw data files in inst/extdata; mapping and assumption files; moirai outputs for land.
OutputsThe full set of input XML files for a GCAM release, plus intermediate 'L' tables you can inspect.
StructureHundreds of chunk functions in a dependency graph; a driver runs them in order.
CodeOpen-source R (JGCRI/gcamdata); Bond-Lamberty et al. (2019).

Where it plugs in

Where it plugs into GCAM

GCAM core
Goes in

Everything that is not a scenario add-on.

Comes back

The input XML files and the calibration that GCAM core needs to start.

How often

Run when inputs, assumptions or the model structure change; most users run GCAM with the XML files shipped in a release.

Note. A scenario study usually does not need gcamdata: it writes a small add-on XML instead. Building a new region does.

Policies are add-on XML files loaded after the main inputs. gcamdata is for changing the base data or the model structure.

The wider family

Smaller tools you will meet in papers. Each does one job for GCAM.

helios

Heating and cooling degree-days from gridded climate data, for GCAM's building demand.

GLORY

Reservoir water-supply curves by basin (cost of storing more water), for GCAM's water supply.

CERF

Sites the power plants GCAM builds on a map, by cost and constraints.

Osiris

Crop-yield responses to climate, used to shock GCAM's agricultural yields.

GCAM-USA

GCAM with the 50 US states as regions; the template for GCAM-KSA and other country versions.

rgcam / gcamreader

R and Python packages that query GCAM's output database.

ambrosia

Food demand model (income- and price-responsive) that sits in GCAM's agriculture.

hector R package

Run Hector on its own from R to test emissions pathways.
